# Level-3.1: Storage correction

Level 3.1 adds the storage term to the CO2 flux: `NEE_L3.1` is `FC` plus the single-point storage
term `SC_SINGLE` from EddyPro. The storage term used is in `SC_SINGLE_gfRMED_L3.1`, and every
record with a measured `FC` has one. Both gas analysers go through the same steps. For the
open-path LI-7500 (`IRGA75`, in progress for FP2026.2), `FC` is the flux corrected at
[Level 1.1](L1.1.md), and an added screen of its concentration readings takes effect with the next
run (see [Drift of the LI-7500 storage term](#drift-of-the-li-7500-storage-term)). The examples
below are from the LI-7200 (`IRGA72`).

`LE` and `H` get no storage term, as in Pastorello et al. (2020). Their Level-3.1 columns `LE_L3.1`
and `H_L3.1` keep the level in the name but equal `LE` and `H`.

## Screening

The single-point storage term is the change in the CO2 concentration at 47 m between two
consecutive records, so errors in the concentration enter it directly. Two steps screen it before
it is added. The values they remove are filled with a rolling median.

- **Next to gaps in the concentration.** After a signal loss the LI-7200 can restart with readings
  far from the real concentration, for example 695 ppm on 21 December 2017, and return to it over
  hours. The return entered the storage term as a large negative flux, while the jump at the
  restart fell into the gap and was never counted. Over 2016 to 2026 these one-sided changes summed
  to about −150 gC m-2. Next to each gap in `CO2_MOLAR_DENSITY`, the storage values are discarded
  until the concentration is back within 3 robust standard deviations of its median at the same
  time of day (±7 days).
- **Spikes.** Isolated spikes, found with a Hampel filter on the double differences, are replaced
  together with one record on either side by the mean of that block. The block keeps its sum, so
  the storage term stays balanced.

After the screening, the cumulative storage term of the LI-7200 at measured fluxes stays close to
zero, except in December 2017 to January 2018 and in November 2020, when the analyser was failing.
The fluxes of these periods are rejected by the signal strength test or removed by hand at
Level 3.2, so their storage term does not enter the flux. For the LI-7500 the cumulative storage
term does not stay close to zero in the current outputs (see
[Drift of the LI-7500 storage term](#drift-of-the-li-7500-storage-term)). The numbers of each run
are in the notebook.

## Drift of the LI-7500 storage term

Over a year a storage term should sum to about zero. The annual sums of `SC_SINGLE_gfRMED_L3.1` are:

| | LI-7500 | LI-7200 |
|---|---|---|
| All years | −25 to −196 (2004-2017) | −23 to +15 |
| Overlap, 11 January 2016 to 12 December 2017 | −159 | +0.7 |

: Annual sums of the storage term, gC m-2 yr-1, in the current outputs. {#tbl-l31-storage-sums}

**Cause.** In the overlap, the dry CO2 mixing ratio of the LI-7500 was compared with that of the
LI-7200, after removing a calibration offset of +8.7 ppm.

- At an AGC above 55, or after rain, the LI-7500 reads 33 to 106 ppm too high. Without rain and at
  an AGC up to 55 it lies within a few ppm of the LI-7200, also in humid air (−0.8 to −2.2 ppm).
- The high readings lie around its gaps: +47 ppm in the last record before a gap and +86 ppm in the
  first record after it. The reading returns within 3 to 4 records.
- Across its 637 gaps the LI-7500 concentration rises by 12.4 ppm (median), that of the LI-7200 by
  0.1 ppm. The rise falls into the gap and is not counted, while the return after the gap enters
  the storage term as a negative flux. This accounts for about 130 of the 160 gC m-2 yr-1 of the
  drift.

The likely cause is a wet or blocked optical path. Wet or contaminated windows are known to bias the
concentration an open-path analyser reports (Serrano-Ortiz et al., 2008; Heusinkveld et al., 2008),
and a bias of the mean concentration enters the LI-7500 flux through its calibration curve (Fratini
et al., 2014). Without rain the concentration error is about −0.5 %. Through the density correction
and the calibration curve it changes the flux by less than 1 %, so it does not explain the low
nighttime fluxes of the LI-7500 in humid weather (see
[Known issues](Issues.md#nighttime-nee-of-the-li-7500-is-too-low-on-cold-and-humid-nights)).

**Correction.** The screen below is applied in `05_FPPC_IRGA75_NEE_OPCO`, Step 3, since the run of
4 November 2026. A CO2 reading is used for the storage term only:

- at an AGC up to 55;
- without rain in the previous 3 hours (`PREC_TOT_T1_47_1`);
- at a relative humidity below 99.5 %;
- not among the first 3 records after a gap.

The storage values that use another reading are discarded. A run of them between two valid readings
is replaced by its mean, which keeps the concentration change between the two valid readings. The
other discarded values are filled with the rolling median.

**Effect.** With the screen (`CUT_50`) the LI-7500 chain gives:

- storage sums of −5 to +39 gC m-2 yr-1 in 2004-2015 (mean +13), in the test run and in the
  product runs since 4 November 2026;
- annual `NEE` higher by 70 gC m-2 yr-1 in 2004-2015 and by 118 gC m-2 yr-1 in 2016-2017;
- in the overlap, a difference to the LI-7200 smaller by about 120 of about 400 gC m-2 yr-1, and on
  the records both analysers keep, a difference of −2.9 instead of −14.9 gC m-2 yr-1.

**Robustness.**

- Over 54 combinations of the settings (AGC limit 50, 55 or 60; rain in the previous 1, 3 or
  6 hours; with or without the humidity condition; 2, 3 or 4 records skipped after a gap), the mean
  storage sum of 2004-2015 stays within −10 to +9 gC m-2 yr-1.
- Settings derived from 2016 alone (AGC limit 55) or from 2017 alone (50.5) leave the storage term
  of both years within −44 to +12 gC m-2 yr-1 of that of the LI-7200. Without the screen the
  difference is about −500 gC m-2 yr-1.
- A lower AGC limit for the flux itself, 55 or 60 instead of 90, was tested and not adopted. Alone
  it changes the annual sums by −88 to +128 gC m-2 yr-1, with signs that differ between years.

## Known limitations

**Storage from one height.** The storage term comes from the CO2 concentration at one height. CO2
that collects in the canopy air during calm hours and is flushed out when the wind rises is captured
only as far as the concentration at that height shows it. At high `USTAR`, night `NEE` after calm
hours points to such a release, while the storage term is not clearly negative; right above the
USTAR threshold no release shows. The numbers are on [Level 3.3](L3.3.md#threshold-detection) (see
also [Known issues](Issues.md#night-time-nee-keeps-rising-above-the-ustar-thresholds)).
