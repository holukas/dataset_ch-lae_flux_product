# Level-3.1: Storage correction

The storage term is added to the CO2 flux: `NEE_L3.1` is `FC` plus the single-point storage term
`SC_SINGLE` from EddyPro. The storage term used is in `SC_SINGLE_gfRMED_L3.1`, and every record with
a measured `FC` has one. The same steps apply to both gas analysers; for the open-path LI-7500
(`IRGA75`, in progress for FP2026.2) `FC` is the flux corrected at [Level 1.1](L1.1.md). For the
LI-7500 a screen of the concentration readings is added, which takes effect with the next run (see
[Drift of the LI-7500 storage term](#drift-of-the-li-7500-storage-term)). The examples below are
from the LI-7200 (`IRGA72`).

`LE` and `H` get no storage term, as in Pastorello et al. (2020). Their Level-3.1 columns
`LE_L3.1` and `H_L3.1` keep the level in the name but equal `LE` and `H`.

## Screening

The single-point storage term is the change in the CO2 concentration at 47 m between two
consecutive records, so errors in the concentration enter it directly. Two steps screen it before it is
added; the values they remove are filled with a rolling median.

- **Next to gaps in the concentration.** After a signal loss the LI-7200 can restart with readings
  far from the real concentration and return to it over hours, for example 695 ppm on
  21 December 2017. The return entered the storage term as a large negative flux, while the jump
  at the restart fell into the gap and was never counted. Over 2016 to 2026 these one-sided
  changes summed to about −150 gC m-2. Next to each gap in `CO2_MOLAR_DENSITY`, the storage values
  are discarded until the concentration is back within 3 robust standard deviations of its median
  at the same time of day (±7 days).
- **Spikes.** Isolated spikes, found with a Hampel filter on the double differences, are replaced
  together with one record on either side by the mean of that block. The block keeps its sum, so
  the storage term stays balanced.

For the LI-7200, the cumulative storage term at measured fluxes stays close to zero after the
screening, except in December 2017 to January 2018 and in November 2020, when the analyser was
failing. The fluxes of these periods are rejected by the signal strength test or removed by hand at
Level 3.2, so their storage term does not enter the flux. For the LI-7500 it does not stay close to
zero in the current outputs (next section). The numbers of each run are in the notebook.

## Drift of the LI-7500 storage term

Over a year a storage term should sum to about zero. The annual sums of `SC_SINGLE_gfRMED_L3.1` are
−25 to −196 gC m-2 yr-1 for the LI-7500 (2004-2017) and −23 to +15 gC m-2 yr-1 for the LI-7200. In
the overlap of 11 January 2016 to 12 December 2017 they are −159 and +0.7 gC m-2 yr-1.

**Cause.** In the overlap, the dry CO2 mixing ratio of the LI-7500 was compared with that of the
LI-7200, after removing a calibration offset of +8.7 ppm.

- At an AGC above 55, or after rain, the LI-7500 reads 33 to 106 ppm too high. Without rain and at
  an AGC up to 55 it lies within a few ppm of the LI-7200, also in humid air (−0.8 to −2.2 ppm).
- The high readings lie around its gaps: +47 ppm in the last record before a gap and +86 ppm in the
  first record after it. The reading returns within 3 to 4 records.
- Across its 637 gaps the LI-7500 concentration rises by 12.4 ppm (median), that of the LI-7200 by
  0.1 ppm. The rise falls into the gap and is not counted, while the return after the gap enters the
  storage term as a negative flux. This accounts for about 130 of the 160 gC m-2 yr-1 of the drift.

The likely cause is a wet or blocked optical path. Wet or contaminated windows are known to bias
the concentration an open-path analyser reports (Serrano-Ortiz et al., 2008; Heusinkveld et al.,
2008), and a bias of the mean concentration enters the LI-7500 flux through its calibration curve
(Fratini et al., 2014). Without rain the concentration error is about −0.5 %, which changes the flux
by less than 1 % through the density correction and the calibration curve. It therefore does not
explain the low nighttime fluxes of the LI-7500 in humid weather (see
[Known issues](Issues.md#nighttime-nee-of-the-li-7500-is-too-low-on-cold-and-humid-nights)).

**Correction, set for the next run.** The current outputs still contain the drift. The screen below
is set in `05_FPPC_IRGA75_NEE_OPCO`, Step 3, and takes effect with the next run of that notebook. A
CO2 reading is used for the storage term only:

- at an AGC up to 55;
- without rain in the previous 3 hours (`PREC_TOT_T1_47_1`);
- at a relative humidity below 99.5 %;
- not among the first 3 records after a gap.

The storage values that use another reading are discarded. A run of them between two valid
readings is replaced by its mean, which keeps the concentration change between the two valid
readings. The other discarded values are filled with the rolling median.

**Tested effect.** A test run of the LI-7500 chain with the screen (`CUT_50`) gives:

- storage sums of −5 to +39 gC m-2 yr-1 in 2004-2015 (mean +13);
- annual `NEE` higher by 70 gC m-2 yr-1 in 2004-2015 and by 118 gC m-2 yr-1 in 2016-2017;
- in the overlap, a difference to the LI-7200 smaller by about 120 of about 400 gC m-2 yr-1, and on
  the records both analysers keep, a difference of −2.9 instead of −14.9 gC m-2 yr-1.

**Robustness.** Over 54 combinations of the settings (AGC limit 50, 55 or 60; rain in the previous
1, 3 or 6 hours; with or without the humidity condition; 2, 3 or 4 records skipped after a gap), the
mean storage sum of 2004-2015 stays within −10 to +9 gC m-2 yr-1. Settings derived from 2016 alone
(AGC limit 55) or from 2017 alone (50.5) leave the storage term of both years within −44 to
+12 gC m-2 yr-1 of that of the LI-7200; without the screen the difference is about −500 gC m-2 yr-1.

A lower AGC limit for the flux itself, 55 or 60 instead of 90, was tested and not adopted. Alone it
changes the annual sums by −88 to +128 gC m-2 yr-1, with signs that differ between years.

## Known limitations

**Storage drift of the LI-7500 in the current outputs.** Until the next run of
`05_FPPC_IRGA75_NEE_OPCO`, the storage term of the LI-7500 sums to −25 to −196 gC m-2 yr-1, and its
annual `NEE` shows too much uptake. In the test run, the screen raises annual `NEE` by about
70 gC m-2 yr-1 in 2004-2015 and 118 gC m-2 yr-1 in 2016-2017 (see
[above](#drift-of-the-li-7500-storage-term)).

**Storage from one height.** The storage term comes from the CO2 concentration at one height. CO2
that collects in the canopy air during calm hours and is flushed out when the wind rises is captured
only as far as the concentration at that height shows it. At high `USTAR`, night `NEE` exceeds
`RECO_DT` more after calm hours than after windy ones, for the LI-7500 from a `USTAR` of
0.7 m s-1 and for the LI-7200 only above 0.9 m s-1, while the storage term is not clearly negative.
This points to such a release; the calm cases are few. Right above the USTAR threshold, the kept
night records after calm hours read low rather than high, so no release shows there (see
[Level 3.3](L3.3.md#threshold-detection) and
[Known issues](Issues.md#night-time-nee-keeps-rising-above-the-ustar-thresholds)).

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| `NEE_L3.1` | `FC` plus the storage term, µmol m-2 s-1 |
| `SC_SINGLE_gfRMED_L3.1` | Storage term used, µmol m-2 s-1 |
| `FLAG_SC_SINGLE_gfRMED_L3.1_ISFILLED` | 0 measured, 1 filled because missing, 2 spike block replaced by its mean, 3 discarded next to a gap and filled; for the LI-7500 from the next run also discarded because it uses an invalid reading, and replaced by the run mean or filled |
