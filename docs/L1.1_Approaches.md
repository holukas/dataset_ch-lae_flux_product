# Approaches to the open-path correction

The open-path LI-7500 (`IRGA75`) shows too much CO2 uptake, mainly because its sensor head warms
the air in the optical path ([Level 1.1](L1.1.md)). This page sets out the ways such a bias can be
corrected, what each requires, and which of them have been tested at CH-LAE, with the notebooks
that carry the evidence.

::: {.callout-note title="In progress for FP2026.2"}
The investigation of a correction estimated from the LI-7500 data alone started in November 2026.
Its first results are summarised below; they come from scratch analyses, and the notebooks and
figures will be added to this page. FP2026.2 currently uses the correction fitted to the parallel
measurements (opco, [Level 1.1](L1.1.md)).
:::

## Approaches

| Approach | Requires | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Correction of the raw data | 20 Hz data; the temperature in the optical path | Corrects where the error arises | The path temperature is not measured and must be modelled |
| Fit to parallel measurements | A closed- or enclosed-path analyser in parallel | Most reliable where the overlap is | Assumes the instrument behaves the same outside the overlap |
| Detection from the open-path data | Open-path data and standard meteorology | Needs no reference in the target years | Weak signal, confounded by real flux; needs pooling |
| Generic correction from the literature | Instrument geometry, meteorology | Applicable without any reference | Does not transfer between sites |
| No correction, stated uncertainty | Independent constraints, if available | Makes no unsupported assumption | Leaves a known bias in the data |

: Approaches to correcting the self-heating bias of an open-path analyser. {#tbl-l11a-approaches}

**Correction of the raw data.** The density conversion uses the air temperature that the sonic
anemometer measures. Replacing it with the temperature in the optical path, or correcting the
concentration sample by sample, and recomputing the fluxes would remove the error where it arises.
The LI-7500 has no fast temperature measurement in its path, so the warming has to come from a
model whose parameters are taken from one of the other approaches.

**Fit to parallel measurements.** The bias is described by physical terms, here a heating term and
the thermal term of the density correction, whose coefficients are fitted to the difference from
a closed- or enclosed-path analyser measuring in parallel. This is the approach of opco and of the
site-specific fits in the literature [@jarvi_comparison_2009; @kittler_high-quality_2017]. Its
limitation is the transfer in time: applying the coefficients to years without the reference
assumes that the sensor head, its mounting and its calibration behaved as during the overlap.

**Detection from the open-path data.** Self-heating leaves fingerprints that the true flux does not
have: on frozen winter days the daytime flux should equal respiration, the bias decreases with wind
speed, and the warmed path lowers the mean CO2 density the analyser reads. Each can be turned into
an estimate of the size of the heating for a given period. The signal is small against the scatter
of the data and partly confounded with real changes of the flux, so periods have to be pooled and
the estimate validated where a reference exists.

**Combination.** The physical form of the correction and its parameters are taken from the overlap
with the LI-7200, and only a factor that may change between years is detected from the LI-7500
data. Validated in 2016-2017, this keeps the reliability of the fit and removes the assumption that
the early years behaved as the overlap. This is the approach under investigation.

**Generic corrections.** Burba et al. [-@burba_addressing_2008] derive a correction from the
geometry and surface temperatures of the instrument, and Etzold et al. [-@etzold_carbon_2011]
applied a fraction of it at this site. Comparisons with parallel closed-path measurements show that
such corrections do not transfer between sites [@jarvi_comparison_2009; @kittler_high-quality_2017].
The bias may also arise in part from the temperature sensitivity of the analyser's calibration
(Deventer et al., 2021), which scales with the same heat flux and cannot be separated from
self-heating by a comparison of fluxes alone.

**No correction.** Where no approach can be validated, the bias is stated as an uncertainty, and
independent estimates such as forest inventories can bound it.

The tests of November 2026 used the LI-7500 data alone and checked the result against the LI-7200
in 2016-2017:

- **Frozen winter days.** On days below about -2 °C the true daytime flux is close to respiration.
  Measured against the respiration of the LI-7500's own nights at high `USTAR`, the heating part of
  the correction applied to 2004-2015 is about 15 % too large: the factor is 0.84 (range of the
  method variants 0.79-0.88; 16-84 % range 0.69-0.99). It is 0.76 in 2004-2007 and 0.89 in
  2008-2015, a difference within the uncertainty, so there is no step between the two periods. On
  winter days of 2016-2017 the method recovers the factor that matches the LI-7200 to within 0.1.
  The result applies to frozen days, with a low sun and a small `H`; it does not settle the summers.
- **Wind contrast.** The remaining daytime flux of the growing season, regressed on the expected
  heating term, gives a factor of about 0.5, but its test on the LI-7200, where the factor should
  be zero, gives 0.4. Wind changes the true flux too, and the test does not separate the two.
- **Warming of the path.** The mean CO2 and H2O readings of the LI-7500 against the LI-7200 give
  a path warming of about 0 ± 0.5 K, below what the method resolves. The two gases disagree in sign
  and size, which a warming of the path does not allow, and the warm bias of the shielded air
  temperature in sun cannot be separated from it. The test gives no independent estimate.
- **Thermal signals.** The air temperature that EddyPro uses for the LI-7500 runs is the
  analyser's own thermistor, which reads about 2 K above the air at night; the cooler voltage of
  the detector follows it. Neither signal explains the difference to the LI-7200 beyond what the
  meteorology explains. The bias grows in the cold while the warming of the head shrinks, which
  points to a temperature sensitivity of the calibration, or to surfaces the sensors do not see,
  rather than to classic self-heating.
- **Cold nights.** The LI-7500 reads too low on overcast, foggy and calm nights and with a wet
  window. The error lies in the measured covariance, not in the density terms or the calibration,
  and is the same in every year. The correction removes the calm-air part only.
