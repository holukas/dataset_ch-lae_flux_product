# Constraints on the USTAR threshold

The USTAR threshold of CH-LAE is poorly constrained by the data. This page summarizes why, and what
it means for the use of `NEE`, `RECO` and `GPP`. The filter and its thresholds are described under
[Level 3.3](L3.3.md); the evidence is in
[`USTAR_THRESHOLD_DETECTION`](notebooks/30_FLUX_POST-PROCESSING_CHAIN/USTAR_THRESHOLD_DETECTION_2004-2026.09.html).

::: {.callout-note title="Thresholds on this page"}
FP2026.2 uses one set per analyser, detected with the ONEFlux moving point method on its own
complete years (`CUT_16`, `CUT_50`, `CUT_84`): 0.318, 0.435 and 0.546 m s-1 for the LI-7500
(2004-2017), 0.416, 0.526 and 0.647 m s-1 for the LI-7200 (2016-2025). The sets are applied from the
next run of the flux chain; [Level 3.3](L3.3.md) still lists the sets of the run of 1 November
2026, from which `RECO_DT` in @fig-c33-reco-dt comes. The figures are drawn from the outputs of the detection
notebook by the notebook
[`L3.3_CONSTRAINTS_USTAR`](notebooks/95_DOCS_FIGURES/L3.3_CONSTRAINTS_USTAR.html).
:::

## Summary

- Night-time `NEE` increases with `USTAR` up to 0.9 to 1.0 m s-1 and does not reach a plateau at
  any detected threshold.
- With the scatter of these data, the detection methods return thresholds below the true one. The
  thresholds are lower bounds.
- The detected value depends on the method, the record and the amount of data per detection: the
  medians range from 0.30 to 0.67 m s-1.
- The annual thresholds are higher from 2018 than before, because the LI-7200 keeps more of the
  windy nights than the LI-7500.
- No independent reference fixes the threshold: the respiration of the daytime partitioning depends
  on it.
- Annual `NEE` is therefore likely biased towards uptake, by an amount the tower data cannot
  determine.

## Night-time NEE does not reach a plateau

A threshold assumes that above it, night-time `NEE` at a given temperature no longer depends on
`USTAR`. At CH-LAE, night-time `NEE` increases with `USTAR` in every temperature class and in both
records, up to about 0.9 to 1.0 m s-1 and in the warmest classes beyond (@fig-c33-nee-ta). Neither
the FP2026.1 nor the FP2026.2 threshold marks the start of a plateau.

![Mean night-time `NEE` (`SW_IN` below 10 W m-2, after Level 3.2, before the USTAR filter) per
0.1 m s-1 class of `USTAR` and five classes of air temperature, for the record of each analyser. The
shaded band spans `CUT_16` to `CUT_84` of FP2026.2 for that analyser, the solid line is its
`CUT_50`, the dashed line the `CUT_50` of FP2026.1.](images/USTAR_night_NEE_by_TA_class.png){#fig-c33-nee-ta}

Two processes may contribute to the increase above any threshold:

- **Wind-driven efflux.** Wind can draw CO2 out of the soil and the litter. This part of the
  increase is a real flux.
- **Canopy flushing.** At the same `USTAR`, night-time `NEE` exceeds the daytime-partitioning
  respiration more after calm hours than after windy ones (LI-7500 from 0.7 m s-1, LI-7200 above
  0.9 m s-1). This points to CO2 stored in the canopy air during the calm hours, which the storage
  term from one measurement height does not capture. The cases are few.

The tower data cannot separate these from flux that bypasses the sensor. On the 27° slope of the
site, advection contributes measurably to the carbon budget [@etzold_contribution_2010].

## Detected thresholds depend on method and data

![USTAR thresholds detected at CH-LAE by method and record, from the bootstrap draws of all years
(bar: `CUT_16` to `CUT_84`, for REddyProc the 5th to 95th percentile; dot: median), the thresholds
of FP2026.1 and FP2026.2, and the range of the annual reference thresholds of FLUXNET (`VUT_REF`).
The grey band marks the `USTAR` up to which night-time `NEE` increases.](images/USTAR_thresholds_by_method.png){#fig-c33-methods}

- **Method.** The moving point method gives the lowest thresholds, the change point method the
  highest (@fig-c33-methods). FP2026.2 uses the moving point method in order to keep as many
  measured records as can be defended.
- **Settings.** The FP2026.1 thresholds (0.27 to 0.34 m s-1) came from REddyProc with 30 instead of
  20 `USTAR` classes and a shortened plateau test. With the defaults, the same data give about 0.3
  to 0.6 m s-1 per year.
- **Amount of data.** Pooling 3 or 5 years per detection raises the annual thresholds from about 0.3
  to 0.7 m s-1 to about 0.5 to 0.8 m s-1. With all years pooled, no plateau is found from April to
  September. The thresholds of single years are low partly because the class means of one year are
  noisy and pass the plateau test early.
- **Record.** The record of each analyser gives a different `CUT_50` (0.43 m s-1 for the LI-7500,
  0.53 m s-1 for the LI-7200). On the records both measured in 2016-2017 they agree within
  0.01 m s-1, so the difference comes from the years each record covers, not from the instruments.
  The annual thresholds of FLUXNET show the same pattern: 0.23 to 0.35 m s-1 in 2004-2015, 0.46 to
  0.60 m s-1 in 2016-2024.

## The threshold is higher from 2018

On the record of the site (the LI-7500 to 2015, the LI-7200 from 2016), the annual thresholds
average 0.43 m s-1 in 2004-2017 and 0.55 m s-1 in 2018-2025, a difference larger than the scatter
between years allows (Welch test, p < 0.001). The difference is not a change at the site. Climate,
the processing settings, the measured `USTAR` and the replacement of the sonic anemometer in December
2019 do not explain it, and on the records both analysers measured in 2016-2017 they give the same
threshold. It lies in which nights each analyser keeps: the LI-7500 loses many windy nights to
missing data, low signal strength and spikes, while the LI-7200 keeps more of them, increasingly
after 2018. More kept records at high `USTAR` let the plateau test pass later. Within the LI-7200,
2017 already lies at the later level; 2016 is low because its spring is missing. The annual
threshold, the highest of the four seasonal ones, is also higher after windy winters (2007, 2018,
2020, 2022). Each analyser therefore uses the set of its own record.

## Detection is biased low

Synthetic night-time `NEE` with a known `USTAR` effect, placed on the real `USTAR`, temperature and
gaps of the LI-7200 years, tests whether a method recovers a known threshold. Without noise, all
three methods recover it, the moving point method somewhat high. With the scatter of the real data,
they return too low a threshold when the true one is high or the increase is gradual
(@tbl-c33-synthetic).

| Synthetic USTAR effect | Moving point | Change point | REddyProc |
|---|---|---|---|
| plateau from 0.3 | 0.38 | 0.32 | 0.37 |
| plateau from 0.6 | 0.58 | 0.55 | 0.51 |
| plateau from 0.9 | 0.46 | 0.73 | 0.34 |
| gradual increase, plateau from 0.9 | 0.42 | 0.60 | 0.41 |
| increase up to 1.2 | 0.29 | 0.57 | 0.25 |
| no USTAR effect | 0.21 | none | 0.18 |

: Median annual threshold (m s-1) detected in synthetic night-time `NEE` with the scatter of the real
data ([section 3](notebooks/30_FLUX_POST-PROCESSING_CHAIN/USTAR_THRESHOLD_DETECTION_2004-2026.09.html#does-the-detection-find-a-known-threshold)).
{#tbl-c33-synthetic}

Where night-time `NEE` increases gradually, as at this site, a detected threshold is therefore a
lower bound. Without any `USTAR` effect, the moving point methods still return 0.2 m s-1.

## No independent reference

The respiration of the daytime partitioning (`RECO_DT`, Lasslop et al., 2010, [Level 4.2](L4.2.md))
is estimated from the daytime light response, under strong turbulence. Night-time `NEE` reaches it
at a `USTAR` of 0.6 to 0.9 m s-1 and exceeds it above, by up to about 60 % (@fig-c33-reco-dt).

![Mean night-time `NEE` divided by `RECO_DT` of the same half-hours, per class of `USTAR`, before the
USTAR filter, for the warm and the cold half of the year. `RECO_DT` from the run of 1 November 2026.
Lines as in @fig-c33-nee-ta.](images/USTAR_night_NEE_vs_RECO_DT.png){#fig-c33-reco-dt}

`RECO_DT` is not independent of the threshold. It takes its temperature sensitivity from the night
records that pass the USTAR filter, so it rises when the chain applies a higher threshold. With the
FP2026.1 thresholds in the chain, the kept nights agreed with `RECO_DT` near `CUT_16`; with the
higher thresholds of the 1 November run, near `CUT_84` (LI-7200) and `CUT_50` (LI-7500). The
comparison therefore cannot confirm or rule out a threshold.

The two partitioning methods depend on the filtered nights in different ways. `RECO_NT` is fitted
to them and takes over their level, which increases with `USTAR`; `RECO_DT` takes only its
temperature sensitivity from them. Part of the difference between the nighttime and the daytime
partitioning (`RECO_NT` against `RECO_DT`, `GPP_NT` against `GPP_DT`) may therefore follow from the
threshold. This has not yet been quantified.

## Records kept and night-time respiration

Every increase of the threshold removes measured nights, and the night-time respiration estimated
from the remaining ones rises (@tbl-c33-kept). The FP2026.2 `CUT_50` lies near 0.43 m s-1 for the
LI-7500 and 0.53 m s-1 for the LI-7200.

## Implications for data use

- **Use the three USTAR scenarios.** The spread between `CUT_16`, `CUT_50` and `CUT_84` reflects the
  uncertainty of the detection only. The structural uncertainty described on this page is not
  included; treat the spread as a lower limit.
- **Annual `NEE` is likely biased towards uptake.** If the true threshold lies above the detected
  one, the kept nights still underestimate respiration, and gap-filled `NEE` is too negative. A
  higher threshold, in turn, keeps nights that may include flushed canopy CO2 and would overestimate
  respiration. The tower data cannot determine the size of the bias.
- **Compare the two partitionings with care.** A difference between `RECO_NT` and `RECO_DT` may in
  part reflect the threshold rather than the partitioning models.
- **Different thresholds per analyser.** The LI-7200 years are filtered at a higher threshold than
  the LI-7500 years. Part of a difference in night-time `NEE` between the two periods may therefore
  arise from the USTAR filter.
- **Night-time `NEE` is mostly gap-filled.** At `CUT_50`, about a fifth of the night half-hours stay
  measured (@tbl-c33-kept).
