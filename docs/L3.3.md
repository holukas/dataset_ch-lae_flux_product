# Level-3.3: USTAR filtering

Level 3.3 flags the `NEE` records of low turbulence. A record with a friction velocity `USTAR`
below the threshold gets flag 2, by day and by night; unlike ONEFlux, the first record above the
threshold is kept. The filter is run at three constant thresholds, which give three USTAR
scenarios: `CUT_16`, `CUT_50` and `CUT_84`. The flags enter the overall quality flag
([QCF](QCF.md)) of each scenario.

`CUT_50` is the best estimate. `CUT_16` and `CUT_84` bound it: the lower threshold keeps more
records of low turbulence, the higher one removes more. Their difference gives the USTAR
uncertainty of [Level 4.1](L4.1.md). The same thresholds are used for every year of an analyser.

## Threshold detection

The thresholds of FP2026.2 come from the moving point method of ONEFlux (Papale et al., 2006), as
diive implements it (`UstarThresholdDetection`, `mode='oneflux_mp'`). For each year, 100 bootstrap
draws of the night records (`SW_IN` below 10 W m-2) give 100 thresholds. The constant thresholds are
the 16th, 50th and 84th percentiles of the draws of all years. The detection runs separately for each
analyser, over its full record, on `NEE` after [Level 3.2](L3.2.md) with quality flags 0 and 1.
Level 3.2 does not depend on the thresholds, so the notebook runs Levels 2 to 3.2 of both chains
itself, with the settings of the chain notebooks, and the detection comes before the chain. Its
Level-3.2 `NEE` is identical to that of the chain notebooks, and it checks that the chain run
applied the thresholds of FP2026.2.

**Why the moving point method.** Of the methods that detect a threshold at this site, it gives the
lowest one. The change point method of ONEFlux (Barr et al., 2013) and the combination of both, which
FLUXNET uses, give higher thresholds, and the REddyProc variant gives about the same. The aim is to
keep as many records as can be defended, because every increase of the threshold removes measured
nights: at the median thresholds of FP2026.2 about a fifth of the night half-hours stay measured.

**One constant set per analyser.** The annual thresholds of single years differ by no more than the
bootstrap spread within one year: for both analysers, the standard deviation between years is below
the mean bootstrap standard deviation of a year. A threshold per year (VUT) would add scatter from
year to year without a detectable change behind it
([section 8](notebooks/30_FLUX_POST-PROCESSING_CHAIN/USTAR_THRESHOLD_DETECTION_2004-2026.09.html#pooling-years-and-seasons)).

**Pooling is not used.** The detection sees the data of one year at a time. Pooling the data of 3 or
5 years before the detection raises the thresholds, from about 0.3 to 0.7 m s-1 for single years to
about 0.5 to 0.8 m s-1. With all years pooled, the moving point method finds no plateau from April to
September, for either analyser. Pooling across seasons (a leaf-off and a leaf-on season, or one
season) gives no consistent threshold: for the LI-7500 with one season the main run finds none, and
the bootstrap draws give 0.13 m s-1, below the threshold of every single year. The single-year
thresholds are therefore low partly because the class means of one year are noisy and pass the
plateau test early. A threshold from pooled data is not better defined, only higher.

**The thresholds are low, and the data cannot fix a higher one.** Night-time `NEE` does not level
off at the thresholds. At the same air temperature it keeps rising with `USTAR` up to about 0.9 to
1.0 m s-1, for both analysers. Tests on synthetic data show that all detection methods return too
low a threshold when the scatter is as large as in these data and `NEE` rises gradually.

The respiration of the daytime partitioning (`RECO_DT`, Lasslop et al., 2010, [Level 4.2](L4.2.md))
does not give an independent reference. Its temperature sensitivity comes from the night records
that pass the USTAR filter, so it rises with the threshold of the chain run. With the thresholds of
FP2026.1 the kept nights agreed with `RECO_DT` at about `CUT_16`; with those of FP2026.2, at about
`CUT_84` for the LI-7200 and just above `CUT_50` for the LI-7500. In the current run the nights kept
at `CUT_50` lie a few percent below `RECO_DT`, and night `NEE` divided by `RECO_DT` keeps rising with
`USTAR` above 1
([section 9](notebooks/30_FLUX_POST-PROCESSING_CHAIN/USTAR_THRESHOLD_DETECTION_2004-2026.09.html#night-nee-against-the-respiration-of-the-daytime-partitioning)).
The comparison therefore cannot confirm or rule out a threshold.

Part of the rise may be real, because wind can draw CO2 out of the soil and the litter. For the
LI-7500, part of the high night `NEE` at high `USTAR` may be CO2 flushed out of the canopy air after
calm hours, which the storage term from one height does not capture; the LI-7200 shows no such
effect, and the calm cases are few
([section 10](notebooks/30_FLUX_POST-PROCESSING_CHAIN/USTAR_THRESHOLD_DETECTION_2004-2026.09.html#night-nee-at-high-ustar-after-calm-hours)).
The night-time respiration that the data give therefore depends on the threshold (see
[Known issues](Issues.md#night-time-nee-keeps-rising-above-the-ustar-thresholds)).

**By day as well.** REddyProc applies the filter only at night; ONEFlux and this chain apply it by
day too. Under the same light, temperature, `VPD` and season, daytime `NEE` at low `USTAR` shows less
uptake in sun, and more at dawn and dusk, than at high `USTAR`. These records are therefore removed
as well.

## Thresholds of FP2026.1

FP2026.1 used one set of thresholds for both analysers. They came from two runs of the R package
REddyProc, one on the LI-7200 data of 2016-2024 and one on the LI-7500 data of 2005-2019; for each
scenario the higher of the two values was used.

| Analyser | Period | Nighttime records used | REddyProc settings changed from default | `CUT_16` | `CUT_50` | `CUT_84` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LI-7200 (`IRGA72`) | 2016-2024 | QCF 0 and 1 | `ustPlateauFwd = 9` (10), `ustPlateauBack = 5` (6), `UstarClasses = 30` (20) | 0.271620 | 0.301811 | 0.333478 |
| LI-7500 (`IRGA75`) | 2005-2017, 2019 | QCF 0 | `ustPlateauFwd = 8` (10), `ustPlateauBack = 4` (6), `UstarClasses = 30` (20) | 0.268384 | 0.303628 | 0.339684 |
| **Used** | | | | **0.271620** | **0.303628** | **0.339684** |

: USTAR thresholds (m s-1) detected for FP2026.1, and the thresholds used. Defaults in brackets.
{#tbl-l33-detection}

These thresholds are low because of the changed settings. The plateau test compares a `USTAR` class
with a fixed number of following classes. With 30 classes instead of 20, and a shorter window, these
cover a narrower range of `USTAR`, so night `NEE` that still rises slowly passes the test as a
plateau. With the REddyProc defaults, the same input gives thresholds of about 0.3 to 0.6 m s-1 per
year. diive repeats both runs exactly, with their settings and with the defaults (notebook above).

## Latent and sensible heat flux

`LE` (and therefore `ET`) and `H` are not filtered for USTAR, following Pastorello et al. (2020):
it is not established that the advection that biases the CO2 flux at low turbulence also affects
the energy fluxes, which are small at night when advection is largest. Their Level-3.3 columns
carry `CUT_NONE` in the name, for example `LE_L3.1_L3.3_CUT_NONE_QCF`.
