# Level-3.3: USTAR filtering

Level 3.3 flags the `NEE` records of low turbulence. A record with a friction velocity `USTAR`
below the threshold gets flag 2, by day and by night; unlike ONEFlux, the first record above the
threshold is kept. The filter runs at three constant thresholds, one per USTAR scenario: `CUT_16`,
`CUT_50` and `CUT_84`. The flags enter the overall quality flag ([QCF](QCF.md)) of each scenario.

`CUT_50` is the best estimate. `CUT_16` and `CUT_84` bound it: the lower threshold keeps more
records of low turbulence, the higher one removes more. Their difference gives the USTAR
uncertainty of [Level 4.1](L4.1.md). Each analyser uses the same thresholds in every year.

The FP2026.2 sets in @tbl-l33-scenarios are those of the current data. The next run uses sets
detected on the complete years of each analyser: 0.318, 0.435 and 0.546 m s-1 for the LI-7500
(2004-2017) and 0.416, 0.526 and 0.647 m s-1 for the LI-7200 (2016-2025)
([Threshold constraints](L3.3_Constraints.md)).

## Threshold detection

The FP2026.2 thresholds come from the moving point method of ONEFlux (Papale et al., 2006), as
diive implements it (`UstarThresholdDetection`, `mode='oneflux_mp'`). For each year, 100 bootstrap
draws of the night records (`SW_IN` below 10 W m-2) give 100 thresholds. The constant thresholds
are the 16th, 50th and 84th percentiles of the draws of all years. The detection runs separately
for each analyser, over its full record, on `NEE` after [Level 3.2](L3.2.md) with quality flags 0
and 1. Level 3.2 does not depend on the thresholds, so the detection notebook runs Levels 2 to 3.2
of both chains itself, before the chain, with the settings of the chain notebooks and an identical
Level-3.2 `NEE`. It also checks that the chain run applied the FP2026.2 thresholds.

**Why the moving point method.** Of the methods that detect a threshold at this site, it gives the
lowest one. The change point method of ONEFlux (Barr et al., 2013) and the combination of both,
which FLUXNET uses, give higher thresholds; the REddyProc variant gives about the same. The aim is
to keep as many records as can be defended, because every increase of the threshold removes
measured nights. At the median thresholds of FP2026.2 about a fifth of the night half-hours stay
measured.

**One constant set per analyser.** For both analysers, the standard deviation of the annual
thresholds between years is below the mean bootstrap standard deviation within a year. A threshold
per year (VUT) would add scatter from year to year without a detectable change behind it
([section 8](notebooks/30_FLUX_POST-PROCESSING_CHAIN/USTAR_THRESHOLD_DETECTION_2004-2026.09.html#pooling-years-and-seasons)).

**The two sets differ by record, not by instrument.** Both Level-1 runs take `USTAR` from the same
sonic anemometer, and their `USTAR` is identical. On the records both analysers measured in
2016-2017, the moving point method gives the same `CUT_50` within 0.01 m s-1 (0.311 m s-1 for the
LI-7200, 0.301 m s-1 for the LI-7500), and night `NEE` rises with `USTAR` in the same way in both.
The two sets therefore differ because of the years each record covers. One record of the site, the
LI-7500 from 2004 to 2015 and the LI-7200 from 2016 to 2025, gives 0.352, 0.486 and 0.599 m s-1,
with a year-to-year standard deviation (0.08 m s-1) within the bootstrap spread of a year
(0.10 m s-1). This set is computed but not adopted; the chain uses the per-analyser sets of
@tbl-l33-scenarios
([section 11](notebooks/30_FLUX_POST-PROCESSING_CHAIN/USTAR_THRESHOLD_DETECTION_2004-2026.09.html#one-threshold-set-for-both-analysers)).

**Pooling is not used.** The detection sees the data of one year at a time.

- Pooling 3 or 5 years before the detection raises the thresholds, from about 0.3 to 0.7 m s-1 for
  single years to about 0.5 to 0.8 m s-1.
- With all years pooled, the moving point method finds no plateau from April to September, for
  either analyser.
- Pooling across seasons (a leaf-off and a leaf-on season, or one season) gives no consistent
  threshold. For the LI-7500 with one season the main run finds none, and the bootstrap draws give
  0.13 m s-1, below the threshold of every single year.

The single-year thresholds are therefore low partly because the class means of one year are noisy
and pass the plateau test early. A threshold from pooled data is not better defined, only higher.

**The thresholds are low, and the data cannot fix a higher one.** At the same air temperature,
night-time `NEE` does not level off at the thresholds but keeps rising with `USTAR` up to about 0.9
to 1.0 m s-1, for both analysers. Tests on synthetic data show that all detection methods return
too low a threshold when the scatter is as large as in these data and `NEE` rises gradually.

The respiration of the daytime partitioning (`RECO_DT`, Lasslop et al., 2010,
[Level 4.2](L4.2.md)) is not an independent reference. Its temperature sensitivity comes from the
night records that pass the USTAR filter, so it rises with the threshold of the chain run. With the
FP2026.1 thresholds the kept nights agreed with `RECO_DT` at about `CUT_16`; with the FP2026.2
thresholds, at about `CUT_84` for the LI-7200 and just above `CUT_50` for the LI-7500. In the
current run the nights kept at `CUT_50` lie a few percent below `RECO_DT`, and night `NEE` divided
by `RECO_DT` keeps rising with `USTAR` above 1
([section 9](notebooks/30_FLUX_POST-PROCESSING_CHAIN/USTAR_THRESHOLD_DETECTION_2004-2026.09.html#night-nee-against-the-respiration-of-the-daytime-partitioning)).
The comparison therefore cannot confirm or rule out a threshold.

Two processes may contribute to the rise:

- Wind can draw CO2 out of the soil and the litter, so part of the rise may be real.
- Part of the high night `NEE` at high `USTAR` may be CO2 flushed out of the canopy air after calm
  hours, which the storage term from one height does not capture. After calm hours night `NEE`
  exceeds `RECO_DT` more than after windy ones, for the LI-7500 from a `USTAR` of 0.7 m s-1 and for
  the LI-7200 only above 0.9 m s-1, while the storage term is not clearly negative. The calm cases
  are few
  ([section 10](notebooks/30_FLUX_POST-PROCESSING_CHAIN/USTAR_THRESHOLD_DETECTION_2004-2026.09.html#night-nee-at-high-ustar-after-calm-hours)).
  Right above the threshold the kept night records after calm hours read low, not high: 0.92 to
  0.96 of `RECO_NT`, the respiration of the nighttime partitioning, at the `CUT_50` of the record of
  the site, against 1.02 to 1.10 after mixed hours. No release of stored CO2 shows there
  ([section 11](notebooks/30_FLUX_POST-PROCESSING_CHAIN/USTAR_THRESHOLD_DETECTION_2004-2026.09.html#one-threshold-set-for-both-analysers)).

The night-time respiration that the data give therefore depends on the threshold (see
[Known issues](Issues.md#night-time-nee-keeps-rising-above-the-ustar-thresholds)).

**By day as well.** REddyProc applies the filter only at night; ONEFlux and this chain apply it by
day too. Under the same light, temperature, `VPD` and season, daytime `NEE` at low `USTAR` shows
less uptake in sun, and more at dawn and dusk, than at high `USTAR`. These records are therefore
removed as well.

## Thresholds of FP2026.1

FP2026.1 used one set of thresholds for both analysers. They came from two runs of the R package
REddyProc, one on the LI-7200 data of 2016-2024 and one on the LI-7500 data of 2005-2019. For each
scenario the higher of the two values was used.

| Analyser | Period | Nighttime records used | REddyProc settings changed from default | `CUT_16` | `CUT_50` | `CUT_84` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LI-7200 (`IRGA72`) | 2016-2024 | QCF 0 and 1 | `ustPlateauFwd = 9` (10), `ustPlateauBack = 5` (6), `UstarClasses = 30` (20) | 0.271620 | 0.301811 | 0.333478 |
| LI-7500 (`IRGA75`) | 2005-2017, 2019 | QCF 0 | `ustPlateauFwd = 8` (10), `ustPlateauBack = 4` (6), `UstarClasses = 30` (20) | 0.268384 | 0.303628 | 0.339684 |
| **Used** | | | | **0.271620** | **0.303628** | **0.339684** |

: USTAR thresholds (m s-1) detected for FP2026.1, and the thresholds used. Defaults in brackets.
{#tbl-l33-detection}

The changed settings made these thresholds low. The plateau test compares a `USTAR` class with a
fixed number of following classes. With 30 classes instead of 20 and a shorter window, these
classes cover a narrower range of `USTAR`, so night `NEE` that still rises slowly passes the test as
a plateau. With the REddyProc defaults, the same input gives thresholds of about 0.3 to 0.6 m s-1
per year. diive repeats both runs exactly, with their settings and with the defaults (in
`USTAR_THRESHOLD_DETECTION`).

## Latent and sensible heat flux

`LE` (and therefore `ET`) and `H` are not filtered for USTAR, following Pastorello et al. (2020).
It is not established that the advection that biases the CO2 flux at low turbulence also affects
the energy fluxes, which are small at night when advection is largest. Their Level-3.3 columns
carry `CUT_NONE` in the name, for example `LE_L3.1_L3.3_CUT_NONE_QCF`.
