## What is corrected

The CO2 flux of the LI-7500 shows too much uptake, for two reasons:

- **Self-heating.** The sensor head warms the air in the optical path. The sonic anemometer does
  not see this heat, so the density (WPL) correction misses it. The error is largest by day and in
  the cold.
- **A switching error.** In some weeks, mostly wet and near-freezing ones, the LI-7500
  over-corrects the thermal dilution, in proportion to the sensible heat flux `H`. In other weeks
  the error is absent. Its cause is not known.

On the half-hours used for training in 2016-2017, on which both analysers passed the quality tests,
the uncorrected LI-7500 flux is on average 1.9 µmol m-2 s-1 below the LI-7200 flux.

## How

The bias of each half-hour is modelled as two physical terms, the heating term and the WPL thermal
term. Each is scaled by a coefficient learned from the parallel measurements with the LI-7200 in
2016-2017. Both coefficients depend on air temperature, incoming shortwave radiation, `USTAR`, wind
speed and whether it is day or night. The coefficient of the WPL thermal term also depends on a
weekly detector, computed from the LI-7500 data alone, which tells the model whether the switching
error was present. The model can therefore be applied to years without the LI-7200. It corrects
2004-2017 and 2019. It replaces the earlier scaling correction (SCOP), whose notebooks are retired.

VPD is not a driver of the coefficients. Near saturation the humidity probe used to 2015 (Rotronic
MP101A) and the one used from 2016 (Campbell CS215) read differently, and both are accurate to only
about 2 % RH there. With VPD as a driver, the model learned this difference between the probes
rather than the error of the analyser, and corrected the cold nights of 2004-2015 by only about a
third of the 2016-2017 value.

**Daytime heating before 2008.** Before 1 January 2008 the daytime part of the heating term is
multiplied by 0.6. The night part and the WPL thermal term are not scaled. The scale rests on
three tests:

- A comparison of the 20 Hz raw data with a quantum cascade laser in October 2005 gives a heating
  effect of 0.4 to 0.7 of the size learned in 2016-2017.
- The same comparison gives about 0.8 (0.45-1.2) in May-June 2008, and 0.9-1.0 against the
  LI-7200 in 2016-2017.
- A summer wind test on the LI-7500 data alone gives about 0.2 for 2005-2007 and about 1 from
  2008.

The switch is placed at the recalibration of the analyser in December 2007. The raw summer
covariances of CO2 and H2O step up once between 2007 and 2008 and do not change further in
2008-2010. The scale is included in `BIAS_OPCO`, `BIAS_HEAT_OPCO` and `BIAS_HEAT_DAY_OPCO`. The
coefficients are those of the fitted model. The correction at the size learned in 2016-2017 is
recovered by dividing `BIAS_HEAT_DAY_OPCO` before 2008 by 0.6. The evidence is summarised in
[FLUX_NEE_IRGA75_EVALUATION](notebooks/90_OVERVIEW/FLUX_NEE_IRGA75_EVALUATION.html#self-heating-scale-per-year).

`FC` stays the uncorrected flux. The corrected flux is `FC_L1.1_OPCO`; the bias, its two parts and
the coefficients are in the columns ending in `_OPCO`. Half-hours where a term of the bias cannot
be computed have no corrected flux.

## Known limitations

- **Cold, foggy days.** On days near 0 °C with freezing fog, the correction falls short, on single
  days by several µmol m-2 s-1 (late December 2016). The likely cause is rime or frost on the
  sensors. The error is episodic, and the model cannot learn it.
- **Cold and humid nights.** In 2016-2017, below 5 °C and at 99.5 % RH or more, the median
  nighttime `NEE` of the LI-7500 after all quality tests is +0.9 µmol m-2 s-1, with 39 % negative
  values; the LI-7200 has +2.5, with 10 % negative values. On calm, cold nights (wind below
  2 m s-1, below 0 °C) the corrected flux remains about 1 µmol m-2 s-1 below the LI-7200.
  [Level 3.2](L3.2.md) removes the worst fog episodes by hand and nights of apparent uptake by a
  test of whole nights. The bias lies in the measured covariance, not in the density correction.
  It occurs on overcast or foggy nights, in calm air and with a wet window, and is the same in every
  year; the correction removes most of the calm-air part only. In 2004-2015 it makes the annual
  `NEE` about 53 gC m-2 per year too negative. See
  [Issues](Issues.md#nighttime-nee-of-the-li-7500-is-too-low-on-cold-and-humid-nights).
- **2016 and 2017 are training years**, so their correction is in sample.
- **2019** comes from another LI-7500 head than the training years. The merged record of FP2026.2
  does not use it.
- **2006 and 2007.** In frozen conditions the corrected flux still depends on `H`. In the other
  years the correction removes most of that dependence. The cause is not known: the EddyPro
  settings and the time lags of these years are the same as in the other years.
- **Daytime flux of 2004-2007.** The size of the heating effect before 2008 is uncertain. The
  scale of 0.6 lies inside the range of 0.4 to 1.0 measured in the raw data. That range is the
  stated uncertainty of the annual `NEE` of these years (@tbl-opco-scale). It is larger than the
  `USTAR` range.
- **Remaining daytime gap.** With the scale, the daytime flux of the growing seasons of 2004-2006
  and of 2008-2009 still lies about 180 to 220 gC m-2 per year above that of the LI-7200 years.
  The checks of the analyser, the raw data and the calibration found no measurement fault. Whether
  the forest took up less CO2 in these years cannot be decided without outside data. See
  [Comparisons of the LI-7500 years](IRGA75_Comparisons.md#does-the-correction-hold-for-the-early-years)
  and [Issues](Issues.md#biases-of-the-li-7500-nee-against-a-climate-baseline).
- **Calibration of 2004-2010.** The LI-7500 read CO2 6 to 44 ppm too low in 2004-2007 and H2O
  about 30 % too high from May 2008 to December 2010. Neither is corrected in FP2026.2; see
  [Issues](Issues.md#calibration-of-the-li-7500-in-2004-2010).
- **Nights with a strongly positive `H`** (about +100 W m-2) get a correction of about
  +3 µmol m-2 s-1. Such `H` values may come from a wet sonic.
- **Only the total bias is meaningful.** By day its two parts, and the two coefficients, cannot be
  separated. Use `BIAS_OPCO`, not its parts.
