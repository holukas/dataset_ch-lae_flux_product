## What is corrected

The CO2 flux of the LI-7500 shows too much uptake, for two reasons:

- **Self-heating.** The sensor head warms the air in the optical path. The sonic anemometer does
  not see this heat, so the density (WPL) correction misses it. The error is largest by day and in
  the cold.
- **A switching error.** In some weeks, mostly wet and near-freezing ones, the LI-7500
  over-corrects the thermal dilution, in proportion to the sensible heat flux `H`. In other weeks
  the error is absent. Its cause is not known.

On the half-hours used for training in 2016-2017 (both analysers passed the quality tests), the
uncorrected LI-7500 flux is on average 1.9 µmol m-2 s-1 below the LI-7200 flux.

## How

The bias of each half-hour is modelled as two physical terms, the heating term and the WPL thermal
term, each scaled by a coefficient learned from the parallel measurements with the LI-7200 in
2016-2017. Both coefficients depend on air temperature, incoming shortwave radiation, `USTAR`, wind
speed and whether it is day or night. The coefficient of the WPL thermal term also depends on a
weekly detector, computed from the LI-7500 data alone, which tells the model whether the switching
error was present. The model can therefore be applied to years without the LI-7200. It corrects
2004-2017 and 2019. This method replaces the earlier scaling correction (SCOP); its notebooks are
retired.

VPD is not a driver of the coefficients. Near saturation the humidity probe used to 2015 (Rotronic MP101A) and the one
used from 2016 (Campbell CS215) read differently, and both are accurate to only about 2 % RH there.
With VPD as a driver, the model learned this difference between the probes rather than the error of
the analyser, and corrected the cold nights of 2004-2015 by only about a third of the 2016-2017
value.

`FC` stays the uncorrected flux. The corrected flux is `FC_L1.1_OPCO`; the bias, its two parts and
the coefficients are in the columns ending in `_OPCO`. Half-hours where a term of the bias cannot be
computed have no corrected flux.

## Result

On the training half-hours of 2016-2017, the uncorrected flux sums to 552 gC m-2 less than the
LI-7200 flux. The corrected flux leaves -7 gC m-2: -13 gC m-2 by day and +6 gC m-2 at night. Out of
sample, with each 14-day block predicted by a model fitted on the other blocks, it leaves
-2 gC m-2. What remains does not depend on light.

## Known limitations

- **Cold, foggy days.** On days near 0 °C with freezing fog, the correction falls short, on single
  days by several µmol m-2 s-1 (late December 2016). The likely cause is rime or frost on the
  sensors. The error is episodic, and the model cannot learn it.
- **Cold and humid nights.** Nighttime `NEE` of the LI-7500 falls with humidity and cold: below
  5 °C and at 99.5 % RH or more its median is about -0.1 µmol m-2 s-1, and half of the values are
  negative. The LI-7200 stays near +2 µmol m-2 s-1, with about 10 % negative values, in every
  humidity class. On calm, cold nights (wind below 2 m s-1, below 0 °C) the corrected flux remains
  about 1 µmol m-2 s-1 below the LI-7200. At [Level 3.2](L3.2.md) the worst fog episodes are
  removed by hand, and a test of whole nights removes nights of apparent uptake. See
  [Issues](Issues.md).
- **2016 and 2017 are training years**, so their correction is in sample.
- **2019** comes from another LI-7500 head than the training years. The merged record of FP2026.2
  does not use it.
- **2006 and 2007.** In frozen conditions the corrected flux still depends on `H`. In the other
  years the correction removes most of that dependence. The setup of these two years has not been
  checked.
- **Nights with a strongly positive `H`** (about +100 W m-2) get a correction of about
  +3 µmol m-2 s-1. Such `H` values may come from a wet sonic.
- **Only the total bias is meaningful.** By day its two parts, and the two coefficients, cannot be
  separated. Use `BIAS_OPCO`, not its parts.

The gap-filled annual sums of the corrected flux carry a further uncertainty, from the half-hours
the LI-7500 did not measure. See [Issues](Issues.md).
