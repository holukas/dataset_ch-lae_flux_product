The correction refines the self-heating correction for open-path analysers of earlier studies
(Burba et al., 2006; Järvi et al., 2009; Kittler et al., 2017). It is implemented in the Python
library [diive](https://github.com/holukas/diive) and applies to the CO2 flux only. It has three
steps: an unscaled flux correction term $\text{FCT}_{\text{unsc}}$ from physical relations, a
scaling factor $\text{SF}$ derived against the enclosed-path analyser, and the application of the
scaled term to the flux.

## Physical basis

In sun, the sensor head of the open-path analyser heats up and warms the air in its sampling path.
The open-path analyser measures the CO2 molar density ($q_c$, mol m-3), so the warmer, less dense
air lowers the measured concentration. The result is a negative flux bias, an apparent CO2 uptake.

## 1. Unscaled flux correction term

The correction term is calculated from the heat transfer between the instrument surface and the
air. Three methods are available: Järvi et al. (2009) (`JAR09`), Burba et al. (2006) (`BUR06`) and
the multi-surface approach of Burba et al. (2008) (`BUR08`). For `JAR09` and `BUR06`:

$$\text{FCT}_{\text{unsc}} = \frac{(T_s - T_a) q_c}{r_a T_a K} \left(1 + 1.6077 \frac{\rho_v}{\rho_d}\right)$$

- $T_s$, $T_a$: instrument surface and ambient air temperature. $T_s$ is estimated from $T_a$ with
  the parameterisation of the chosen method.
- $q_c$: CO2 molar density (µmol m-3).
- $r_a$: aerodynamic resistance to heat transfer (s m-1), from wind speed $u$ and friction
  velocity $u_*$.
- $T_a K$: ambient air temperature in kelvin.
- $1 + 1.6077 \rho_v/\rho_d$: water vapour correction factor, analogous to the WPL correction.

Gaps in $\text{FCT}_{\text{unsc}}$, where an auxiliary variable is missing, are filled with a
machine-learning model (a random forest in the existing notebooks), so that the correction can be
applied to every flux.

## 2. Scaling factor

The physical term has to be tuned to the instrument and the site. The enclosed-path LI-7200 is not
affected by self-heating and measured in parallel with the LI-7500 from January 2016 to
December 2017, so its flux serves as the reference. The scaling factor $\xi$ minimises the
L1 norm of the difference between the cumulative sums of the corrected open-path flux and of the
reference flux, taken at every time step:

$$\text{Minimize} \sum \left| \sum \left( \text{Flux}_{\text{OP, raw}} + \text{FCT}_{\text{unsc}} \cdot \xi \right) - \sum \text{Flux}_{\text{CP, ref}} \right|$$

Matching the cumulative sums makes the long-term CO2 budgets of the two analysers agree. The
optimisation is done separately for daytime and nighttime and for classes of a variable such as
$u_*$, because self-heating depends on radiation and turbulence. Circular block bootstrapping
resamples the time series while preserving its autocorrelation, and gives a median scaling factor
with confidence intervals for each class.

## 3. Application

Each half-hour receives the median scaling factor of its class and of day or night. The corrected
flux is:

$$\text{FCT} = \text{FCT}_{\text{unsc\_gf}} \cdot \text{SF}$$

$$\text{Flux}_{\text{OP, corr}} = \text{Flux}_{\text{OP, raw}} + \text{FCT}$$
