Calculate VPD (2004-2026.09)

Modified

27 October 2026

notebook version: 4 (23 Oct 2026)

ℹ️ About this notebook

Computes vapour pressure deficit VPD from the two products that precede it, and stores it to the external data folder. No download, no gap-filling: 02 and 04 must be run first.

VPD follows from TA and RH by formula. Both inputs are complete, so VPD is computed for every record and no model runs here. Both inputs come from one probe at 47 m, which changed twice: from a Rotronic MP101A to a Campbell CS215 on 21 January 2016, and to a Campbell HygroVUE10 on 5 June 2026. Notebooks 02 and 04 each export the measured series and a homogenised one that puts every probe era onto one level. VPD is computed on both bases and both are exported, on the pattern of 02, 04, 08 and 09:

  • VPD_T1_47_1 — from the measured pair, TA_T1_47_1_gfXG and RH_T1_47_1. This is the VPD the probe reported. It is the series that matches the measured TA and RH used as biomet input to EddyPro and as drivers in the flux processing chain. It steps where the probe changed.
  • VPD_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED — from the homogenised pair, TA_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED_gfXG and RH_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED. Use it for anything that crosses January 2016 or June 2026.
  • FLAG_VPD_T1_47_1_ISFILLED — what each record was computed from; 0 = measured TA and measured RH, >0 = one input was gap-filled or reconstructed (see FLAG_LEGEND). The homogenised inputs are filled at the same records as the measured ones, so this flag describes both value columns.
  • FLAG_VPD_T1_47_1_SOURCE — which probe, and which state of it, stands behind the record (the SOURCE flag of 02 and 04, carried over unchanged).

Changes against notebook version 3. Version 3 exported one column, computed from the homogenised TA and the measured RH, because 04 exported no homogenised RH at the time. That mixed basis corrected the temperature term and left the humidity term, and its step at 2016 was the largest of the bases scored below. 04 now exports a homogenised RH, so the mixed basis is computed here only as a diagnostic and is not exported. The column name VPD_T1_47_1 now stands for the measured pair, as TA_T1_47_1_gfXG and RH_T1_47_1 do in their products.

The section on the probe changes scores the three bases against MeteoSwiss Lägern, at January 2016 and at June 2026, and says which of the two comparisons is an independent test.

Helper functions

Three helpers, each with the job of failing loudly rather than quietly doing nothing:

  • load_product() reads one of the input files and refuses anything whose index is not a continuous, duplicate-free 30MIN series or that is missing the columns asked for. Every later step aligns three files on a shared index, and a silently mis-shaped input would produce a VPD full of NaN that still had the right length.
  • short_gap_mask() selects whole gap runs no longer than a limit. Same helper as notebook 02.
  • check_vpd_range() is the physical guard on the result, used twice: on the computed VPD and again on the exported series.
def load_product(filename: str, columns: list[str]) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Load one stage-10 product and verify it is usable as an input here.

    Raises rather than returning a frame that would quietly poison the merge: missing columns, a
    non-30MIN index, duplicate timestamps or an index with holes all stop the notebook.
    """
    filepath = Path(OUTPATH) / f"{filename}.parquet"
    if not filepath.is_file():
        raise FileNotFoundError(
            f"{filepath} does not exist. Run the notebook that produces it before this one.")

    df = dv.load_parquet(filepath=str(filepath))

    missing = [c for c in columns if c not in df.columns]
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"{filename}: expected column(s) {missing}, found {list(df.columns)}.")
    if df.index.duplicated().any():
        raise ValueError(f"{filename}: {int(df.index.duplicated().sum())} duplicate timestamps.")

    steps = df.index.to_series().diff().dropna().unique()
    expected = pd.Timedelta(REQUIRED_TIME_RESOLUTION)
    if len(steps) != 1 or steps[0] != expected:
        raise ValueError(
            f"{filename}: index is not a continuous {REQUIRED_TIME_RESOLUTION} series "
            f"(found step(s) {list(steps)}).")

    out = df[columns].copy()
    print(f"  {filename:<38} {out.index[0]} -> {out.index[-1]}  ({len(out):,} records)")
    return out


def short_gap_mask(series: pd.Series, limit: int) -> pd.Series:
    """True where `series` is missing AND its whole gap is at most `limit` records long.

    Whole runs are selected explicitly. Using pandas' `interpolate(limit=)` instead would fill
    the first `limit` records of a long gap as well, which is not wanted: those values would sit
    on a straight line spanning the entire gap.
    """
    missing = series.isna()
    run_length = missing.groupby((~missing).cumsum()).transform('sum')
    return missing & (run_length <= limit)


def check_vpd_range(series: pd.Series, label: str) -> None:
    """Assert that every value present is plausible VPD in kPa.

    A formula slip or a unit mix-up (hPa instead of kPa) shows up here as a factor of 10, and a
    negative value means either an RH above 100% survived upstream or a model prediction was not
    clipped.
    """
    lo, hi = VPD_PLAUSIBLE_KPA
    values = series.dropna()
    if values.empty:
        raise ValueError(f"{label}: no values at all.")
    if values.min() < lo or values.max() > hi:
        raise ValueError(
            f"{label}: range {values.min():.3f}..{values.max():.3f} kPa is outside the plausible "
            f"{lo}..{hi} kPa. VPD is ~0-4 kPa here; a factor of 10 means hPa.")
    print(f"  {label:<34} n={len(values):>7,}  "
          f"median {float(values.median()):.3f}  range {values.min():.3f}..{values.max():.3f} kPa")

🧮 Calculate VPD

VPD is the difference between the saturation vapour pressure at the given air temperature and the actual vapour pressure, so it follows from TA and RH alone:

\[ \mathrm{VPD} = e_{sat}(T_a)\left(1 - \frac{\mathrm{RH}}{100}\right) \]

diive’s calc_vpd_from_ta_rh() implements this with the Magnus coefficients used by ReddyProc (Kolle Logger Tools Software 2012, valid for water between 0 and 100 °C) and returns kPa:

VPD [hPa] = 6.1078 * (1 - RH/100) * exp(17.08085 * TA / (234.175 + TA))

The formula is applied to two pairs, and each pair is taken as a whole:

  • measured: TA_T1_47_1_gfXG with RH_T1_47_1. One sensor head reports both, so the pair is self-consistent: the relative humidity refers to the temperature reported beside it.
  • homogenised: TA_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED_gfXG with RH_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED. Both are on the level of the normal CS215.

A third pair, the homogenised TA with the measured RH, is the basis of notebook version 3. It is computed as a diagnostic for the scoring section and not exported.

VPD is zero at saturation and never negative. RH arrives from notebook 04 capped at 100 % in both columns, so neither computed series can be negative; check_vpd_range() asserts it.

Provenance of the computed VPD

Every record that has a value at this point was computed by the formula, so none of it is modelled. That is not the same as measured, and this is where the distinction is written down rather than left to be inferred later.

A computed record gets:

  • 0 if both inputs were measurements (TA flag 0 and RH flag 0)
  • 5 if RH was measured but TA came from notebook 02’s gap-filling
  • 6 if RH was reconstructed from the NABEL sensor at 49 m, whatever TA was
  • 7 if RH was reconstructed from MeteoSwiss Lägern, whatever TA was

6 and 7 outrank 5 because it is the larger error: notebook 04 puts about 3 % RH of uncertainty on a reconstructed value, and RH enters the formula linearly, so at typical summer conditions that is a few tenths of a kPa — considerably more than a gap-filled TA contributes.

The homogenised TA and RH are filled and reconstructed at exactly the same records as the measured ones, so this one flag describes both VPD columns.

🔎 What the probe changes do to VPD

TA and RH come from one probe, and both change where the probe changes. In January 2016 the two errors of the MP101A act on VPD in opposition: its TA reads about 1.3 °C too cold, which lowers saturation vapour pressure and pulls VPD down, while its RH reads too dry, which pushes VPD up. A basis that keeps both errors can therefore report a small step by cancellation, and a basis that corrects only one of them a large one. Judging a basis by the size of its step alone would select for the series that hides the most, so each basis is judged together with what it corrects.

The reference is MeteoSwiss Lägern, 2.5 km away, whose own TA and RH give a reference VPD by the same formula. The station lies about 400 m lower than the tower, so a constant offset between the two series is expected and is not evidence; only the change in that offset across a probe change is. Only records computed from two measurements (flag 0) enter, and the residual is split into day and night (sun above or below the horizon), because the shield terms of 02 act only in sunlight.

The two changes are not tested equally.

  • January 2016 is an independent test. 02 and 04 fitted their 2016 transfers against NABEL on the tower, never against Lägern.
  • June 2026 is not. NABEL ends in 2018, so 02 and 04 fitted the HygroVUE10 transfers against Lägern, on the same two samples compared here. A small step of the homogenised basis at June 2026 is therefore expected by construction and is reported, not counted as evidence. What the comparison does show is the size of the change in the measured series.

def ordinary_change(res):
    """Median absolute change in the yearly mean residual between consecutive full years.

    Only the two eras' own records enter, so no later probe state sets the scale. The differences
    that span the changeover are dropped after differencing, rather than the years before it, so
    a 2015 -> 2017 gap cannot masquerade as one ordinary year-to-year change.
    """
    _r = res[(PRE_2016 | POST_2016)[res.index] & (res.index.year <= LAST_FULL_YEAR)]
    _d = _r.groupby(_r.index.year).mean().diff().abs()
    return float(_d.drop(index=[y for y in (BREAK_YEAR, BREAK_YEAR + 1) if y in _d.index]).median())

⚠️ Note for downstream notebooks

Both VPD columns are complete and fully computed: every record follows from TA and RH by the formula; no VPD model runs.

Which column to use.

  • VPD_T1_47_1 is the VPD the probe reported. It matches the measured TA and RH that enter EddyPro and the flux processing chain, and it is the column to pair with them, for example as a gap-filling or partitioning driver. It steps where the probe changed, in January 2016 and in June 2026, so it is not suited to trends or to comparisons across either date.
  • VPD_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED puts every era onto the level of the normal CS215. Use it for anything that crosses January 2016 or June 2026. Its June 2026 transfer rests on one summer and is to be refitted with 02 and 04 once the record holds a winter.

FLAG_VPD_T1_47_1_ISFILLED tells a consumer what each record was computed from, for both columns:

  • 0 — measured TA and measured RH at 47 m. The only records where nothing upstream was modelled or transferred.
  • 5 — measured RH, with a TA that notebook 02 gap-filled. Skilful, but not two measurements.
  • 6 — RH reconstructed from the NABEL sensor at 49 m (notebook 04), ~3 % RH uncertainty.
  • 7 — RH reconstructed from MeteoSwiss Lägern at 2.5 km (notebook 04), a weaker transfer than 6. Almost all after 2018.

FLAG_VPD_T1_47_1_SOURCE names the probe and its state, with the codes of 02 and 04.

The November 2019 outage

One block deserves naming. The 47 m humidity sensor was down from 4 November 2019 04:45 to 19 November 2019 23:45 — 759 consecutive records, 15.8 days, over half that month, by far the longest stretch after 2018 where the tower measured no RH. NABEL had ended by then, so notebook 04 reconstructs it from MeteoSwiss Lägern: the VPD here is computed from a Lägern-reconstructed RH and carries flag 7, not modelled.
