Merge the meteo products and overview (2004-2026.09)

Modified

27 October 2026

notebook version: 7 (27 Oct 2026)

ℹ️ About this notebook

The capstone of the 30_PRODUCTS stage. It reads the per-variable products, joins them onto one continuous 30MIN TIMESTAMP_MID index, writes a single merged file, and draws an overview figure for each series.

Nothing is computed or corrected here. The values are exactly what the product notebooks exported, so this notebook must run after them.

This version covers 2004 to 30 September 2026 and merges all thirteen products. 01 to 07 are extended to September 2026. 08 to 13 (PREC, SWC, TS, G, SW_OUT, LW_OUT) still end on 31 December 2025, so their columns are empty from January 2026 until their notebooks are extended. The merged file therefore holds every meteo series available so far.

Outputs:

  • 99_METEO_MERGED_2004-2026.09.parquet / .csv: every merged value and its provenance flag in one wide table.
  • 99_METEO_MERGED_2004-2026.09_INFO.md: a plain-text description of that table, written beside it.
  • One overview figure per series: daily time series, diurnal fingerprint, annual cycle, and the provenance flag composition per year (or the value distribution where a series carries no flag).

📇 Data sources

All inputs are the parquet products in the external data folder, written by earlier notebooks of this stage. SW_IN, TA and PPFD_IN keep their _gfXG (gap-filled) columns. RH carries a _MISSING flag, LW_IN a _SOURCE flag naming its calibration era, and PA no flag at all. TA, RH, VPD and PREC each contribute two value columns and two flags. The two soil products contribute one entry per depth: 09 five soil-moisture depths with a homogenised twin for four of them, 10 seven soil-temperature depths. 11 contributes a single entry, the reconciled soil heat flux. 12 and 13 are the two outgoing radiation channels: neither is gap-filled, and SW_OUT carries two flags, one naming its instrument and one its screening.

ImportantAir temperature: two value columns and two flags

Two value columns. TA_T1_47_1_gfXG is the gap-filled series as the probes reported it. TA_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED_gfXG puts every probe era onto one level. It raises the Rotronic MP101A era before 2016-01-21 by +1.3197 °C, removes the daytime radiation excess of the Campbell CS215 shield, and transfers the Campbell HygroVUE10 era from 2026-06-05 onto the CS215. Notebook 02 establishes each term, and the TA page lists them per source code. Use the homogenised column for anything crossing 2016-01-21 or 2026-06-05, and the measured column for everything else.

Both flags describe both columns. The pairing in the table above only decides which flag is drawn in that series’ figure: ISFILLED (observed, modelled or reconstructed) for the measured series, SOURCE (which probe and probe state) for the homogenised one, because the probe era is what the homogenisation acts on. All four columns are in the merged file.

Neither TA column contains NaN. Both are complete over the whole period.

ImportantRelative humidity: two value columns and two flags

Two value columns. RH_T1_47_1 is the series as the probes reported it, with its gaps reconstructed from NABEL or MeteoSwiss Lägern. RH_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED puts the MP101A era before 2016 and the HygroVUE10 era from June 2026 onto the level of the CS215, which it leaves unchanged. The 2016 transfer depends on the humidity, so no single constant describes it. The HygroVUE10 transfer is made in vapour pressure and converted back with the homogenised TA of notebook 02, so from June 2026 the homogenised RH refers to that temperature. Notebook 04 establishes both. Use the homogenised column for anything crossing 2016 or June 2026, and the measured column for everything else.

Both flags describe both columns, paired as for TA: MISSING (measured or reconstructed) is drawn for the measured series and SOURCE for the homogenised one. Its codes are those of 02, because one probe delivers both variables.

Neither RH column contains NaN.

ImportantVapour pressure deficit: two value columns and two flags

Two value columns, each computed from one pair. VPD_T1_47_1 is computed from the measured TA and RH, and VPD_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED from their homogenised columns. Notebook 07 computes both by formula and models nothing. Use the homogenised column for anything crossing 2016 or June 2026, and the measured column for everything else, including wherever VPD is paired with the measured TA and RH.

Both flags describe both columns, paired as for TA: ISFILLED (computed from two measurements, or from a filled or reconstructed input) is drawn for the measured series and SOURCE for the homogenised one, with the codes of 02.

Neither VPD column contains NaN.

ImportantPrecipitation: two value columns and two flags

Two value columns. PREC_TOT_T1_47_1 is the measured series; PREC_TOT_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED rescales its pre-2018 half onto the level of its post-2018 half, because the gauge’s acquisition chain changed in mid-2018 and the early era reads ~20% low (notebook 08 establishes this against thirteen MeteoSwiss stations). Use the homogenised column for anything crossing 2018 and the measured column for everything else. They are listed as two entries below so that each gets its own overview figure.

Both flags describe both columns. The pairing in the table above is only about which flag is more informative in that series’ figure: ISFILLED (measured / filled from OED / still missing) for the measured series, SOURCE (which acquisition era and screened record) for the homogenised one, since the era is exactly what the homogenisation acts on. All four columns are in the merged file and apply to both value columns.

PREC contains NaN: 2,224 half-hours before Sep 2014 that have no sub-daily reference to fill them from, and every half-hour after December 2025. Do not read them as dry.

ImportantSoil moisture: one name, two sets of probes

Nine columns from one file. Notebook 09 exports the five depths of the FF1 profile plus a _HOMOGENIZED twin for four of them. 0.5 m has no twin, and that is deliberate: the old profile had no 50 cm probe, so there is a single sensor era and nothing to shift. A duplicate column there would invite someone to difference it, get zero, and conclude the record is homogeneous.

The break is a change of instrument, not of acquisition. Before 2020 these field names carry the old EC-20 profile, screened by the MeteoScreeningTool and stored under the later names; from 2020-04-10 they carry the TEROS 12 profile installed on 19 Mar 2020. The two never share a timestamp, and the level steps by +7.6 to +10.7 % VWC depending on depth. FLAG_..._SOURCE says which sensor set produced each value, and it is the flag for both the measured and the homogenised column.

The homogenisation rests on climatology, not on an overlap. Because the eras never overlap, the pre-2020 half is shifted onto the TEROS level by season-matched monthly offsets rather than by a calibration. Use the homogenised columns for anything crossing 2020; use the measured columns for absolute soil water content, and never the homogenised ones for the pre-2020 era.

2020 mixes both sensor sets in every depth’s annual statistics, and at 0.3 m the measured column additionally steps in March 2025, when the probe was replaced 40 cm down the slope.

ImportantSoil temperature: seven depths, and only the reconciled series

Seven columns from one file, out of thirty-five. Notebook 10 exports one reconciled, gap-filled series per depth and the eighteen individual screened channels behind them. Only the seven reconciled series are merged here: a channel is one sensor, where every other entry in this table is one variable or one depth, and the individual channels are what a reader goes back to notebook 10 for.

Two flags at three depths, and they are not alternatives. METHOD says whether a value was measured — and by which sensor generation — or modelled from the other depths; SUSPECT marks the period between January 2009 and May 2012 in which the early profile’s channels disagreed with one another by 1.5 to 3.5 K. Only 0.05, 0.1 and 0.15 m carry a SUSPECT flag. METHOD is the flag drawn in the figures, because it is the one that says whether the value was measured at all.

Levels are reconciled across April 2020, amplitudes are not. The earlier sensors were more closely coupled to the surface than the present ones at the same nominal depth, so a diurnal or seasonal amplitude computed across the profile replacement compares two different couplings — by a factor of three at 0.05 m. The _HOMOGENIZED in the column name refers to the level only.

Coverage differs by depth far more than for any other product. 0.3 and 0.5 m are about 47 % modelled, essentially all of it the ten-year stretch from March 2010 to April 2020 when no sensor existed at those depths; 0.15 m ends on 2021-03-24 and 0.2 and 0.6 m begin with the 2020 profile, because the fill interpolates within a depth’s own span and never extrapolates beyond it. Filter on METHOD before treating these series as measurements.

The deep depths report on a 0.1 K grid. At 0.5 and 0.6 m the median summer daily amplitude is one reporting step, so a diurnal cycle read off those columns is the instrument’s resolution rather than the soil.

ImportantSoil heat flux: three plates in the file, one series here

One entry from a file of eight columns. Notebook 11 exports the three FF1 plates individually, each with its own SOURCE flag, and then the reconciled column that is merged here. The plates themselves are left out for the same reason 10’s eighteen channels are: a plate is one sensor, where every entry in this table is one variable or one depth. They are also not repeat measurements of a single number. The three sit metres apart beneath a deciduous canopy, so when the leaves are off a patch of sunlight can fall on one and not on the others, and the spread between them describes where that light fell. Notebook 11 therefore exports no plain average of them, and anyone who needs an individual plate goes back to that notebook for it.

The reconciled column carries two corrections, one of scale and one of level. The record is spread over three acquisition setups, and they differ from one another in two separate ways. Their amplitudes differ, and a multiplicative gain per calendar month puts those onto one scale. The earliest setup also has a zero-point error. Over a year the soil returns to about the temperature it started at, so a record of soil heat flux at a few centimetres must average near zero; the two later setups sit within 0.4 W m-2 of zero, and the earliest one has plate means of -3.76 and -3.68 W m-2. Multiplying a series cannot move its zero point, so a second term is needed, and it is one additive constant per plate per setup, fitted on the season-matched level of the gained series. With both terms applied, neither setup boundary leaves a step larger than the same statistic produces at dates well inside a single setup. The gain alone did not manage that: before the additive term was added, the 2012 boundary stepped by more than that statistic ever produces inside a settled setup.

Both terms are fitted on climatology, because there is nothing else to fit them on. The setups never overlap, and no service measures soil heat flux at this plot, so this correction has no independent anchor of the kind notebook 08 had for precipitation. A constant also removes a level and leaves a slope, so a residual drift survives in the early years: an analysis of interannual variability there is reading a real signal plus that drift, and this product cannot separate the two. Use G_FF1_0.05_HOMOGENIZED to cross the 2012 and 2021 boundaries. It is a derived estimate rather than a measurement, and inside a single setup it says nothing the plate columns in notebook 11 do not.

The flag counts plates rather than grading them. FLAG_G_FF1_0.05_HOMOGENIZED_NPLATES says how many of the two contributing plates went into each value, so it takes the values 0, 1 and 2, and what the column represents changes when the count does. The third plate never enters it, because it was discarded before the reference setup began and neither correction term can be fitted for it. The column is empty wherever the count is 0, which happens during forest-floor logger outages and across the 2012 reconfiguration, whose records are real readings that sit on neither setup’s scale and are excluded for that reason. The heat stored in the soil above the plates is not added, so these values are the flux at plate depth and not at the surface.

NoteOutgoing shortwave: which of its two flags the figure draws

SW_OUT carries two flags, and they answer different questions. FLAG_SW_OUT_T1_47_1_INSTRUMENT says which radiometer produced a value and which constant converted it, and FLAG_SW_OUT_T1_47_1_SOURCE says which of the two screenings supplied it. The figure draws the instrument flag, because that is the boundary that changes what a value means: notebook 12 compared the two screenings over the two full years they both cover and found them to be on one scale, so the screening flag is provenance rather than a warning, whereas the December 2021 exchange of the CNR1 for a CNR4 is a real break. Filter on the instrument flag before comparing values across that date, and drop its changeover code, which marks the weeks after the new radiometer went up and before the logger program received its constants. Both flags are in the merged file. LW_OUT needs only one flag, which names its instrument and its calibration factor in a single vocabulary, exactly as 06 does for LW_IN.

def flagcols(flag):
    """Normalise a PRODUCTS flag slot to a list of column names.

    The slot holds None, one name, or a tuple of them. Returning a list from one place keeps
    the merge, the column check, the summary table and the figures from each inventing their
    own handling of the tuple case and drifting apart.
    """
    if flag is None:
        return []
    if isinstance(flag, str):
        return [flag]
    return list(flag)

def figcap(fig, text, width=150):
    """Place a caption under a figure. Call after the last axes of that figure is drawn.

    A figure with a layout engine makes room for a caption itself. A figure without one is
    extended downwards and every axes moved up by the same amount, so the drawing keeps its size
    and the caption gets a free strip below it. Same helper as in the product notebooks.
    """
    width = min(width, max(40, int(14.5 * fig.get_size_inches()[0])))
    caption = textwrap.fill(f"Figure - {text}", width)
    # An engine that still lays the figure out at draw time can make room for a caption itself.
    # plt.tight_layout() does NOT leave one: it executes once and leaves a PlaceHolderLayoutEngine
    # that never runs again, so a caption handed to supxlabel there lands on top of the tick
    # labels. Those figures take the manual branch below.
    _engine = fig.get_layout_engine()
    if _engine is not None and type(_engine).__name__ != 'PlaceHolderLayoutEngine':
        fig.supxlabel(caption, fontsize=9, ha='left', x=0.005)
        return
    lines = len(caption.splitlines())
    extra = 0.17 * lines + 0.12          # inches; one caption line is about 0.17 in at fontsize 9
    width_in, height_in = fig.get_size_inches()
    fig.set_size_inches(width_in, height_in + extra)
    keep = height_in / (height_in + extra)
    offset = extra / (height_in + extra)
    for ax in fig.axes:
        pos = ax.get_position()
        ax.set_position([pos.x0, offset + pos.y0 * keep, pos.width, pos.height * keep])
    fig.text(0.005, 0.01, caption, fontsize=9, ha='left', va='bottom')

📄 The information file

The merged file will be handed to people who have never seen this repository, so it is written with a plain-text description of itself beside it, under the same base name and in the same folder. That placement is the point: a description that lives only on a website is a description the recipient of a data folder may never find.

It is generated here from PRODUCTS, VARIABLE_INFO, FLAG_LABELS and the merged frame itself, rather than written by hand. A hand-written file states the same facts twice and stops being true the first time a variable is added; a generated one cannot disagree with the data it ships with, and the observed ranges in it are read off the exported values rather than typed in.

The file is Markdown, and the table columns are padded so that it also reads as plain text in an editor that renders nothing.

def plain_units(units):
    """The registry's LaTeX units as plain text.

    PRODUCTS stores units as mathtext because the overview figures set them that way. This file has
    to survive being opened in a text editor, where '$^{-2}$' is noise, so the markup is stripped
    here rather than a second unit string being kept beside the first and going stale.
    """
    return units.replace('$', '').replace('^', '').replace('{', '').replace('}', '')


def observed_range(series):
    """The smallest and largest exported value, at a precision that suits the magnitude.

    The precision comes from the data rather than being fixed, because one table here holds air
    pressure near 95 kPa, soil heat flux of a few W m-2 and shortwave radiation above 1000.
    """
    _lo, _hi = float(series.min()), float(series.max())
    _scale = max(abs(_lo), abs(_hi))
    _nd = 2 if _scale < 10 else (1 if _scale < 1000 else 0)
    return f"{_lo:.{_nd}f} to {_hi:.{_nd}f}"


def para(text, width=98):
    """One paragraph, re-wrapped to a width a text editor can show without folding.

    Every paragraph below interpolates something whose length is not known until it is computed -
    a list of series names, a record count - so the wrapping cannot be done by hand in the source
    without a long list eventually running off the line. Wrapping here means the file reads the
    same however many variables it describes.
    """
    return textwrap.fill(' '.join(text.split()), width=width)


def markdown_table(header, rows):
    """A pipe table whose columns line up in a plain-text editor as well as in a Markdown viewer.

    Every column but the last is padded to its widest cell. The last is left ragged on purpose:
    padding a column of sentences out to the longest sentence would push the columns before it off
    the screen, which is the opposite of what the padding is for.
    """
    _w = [max([len(_h)] + [len(_r[_i]) for _r in rows]) for _i, _h in enumerate(header)]

    def _line(cells):
        return "| " + " | ".join([_c.ljust(_w[_i]) for _i, _c in enumerate(cells[:-1])]
                                 + [cells[-1]]) + " |"

    _sep = "|" + "|".join("-" * (_x + 2) for _x in _w[:-1]) + "|" + "-" * (len(header[-1]) + 2) + "|"
    return "\n".join([_line(header), _sep] + [_line(_r) for _r in rows])


def names(shorts):
    """A list of series names as inline code, for a sentence."""
    return ', '.join(f'`{_s}`' for _s in shorts)

## 2. The flag columns

measured or modelled, or which instrument or which processing produced it.

empty as well, because this table spans the whole period from 2004 while several instruments were
installed later. Second, selecting on the flag is how you select the records you want: to work
with measured values only, keep the codes that mean measured and drop the rest, rather than
assuming that a value which is present was measured.

## 3. The timestamp

The index is named `TIMESTAMP_MIDDLE`, and it means what it says: each timestamp marks the
**middle** of the half-hour it summarises, not the start and not the end. The record stamped
`12:15` covers 12:00 to 12:30.

The times are **local standard time, UTC+01:00**, all year round. Daylight saving is never
applied, so in summer a timestamp here is one hour behind the local clock in Switzerland.

The index is continuous. It steps by exactly 30 minutes from 2004-01-01 00:15:00 to 2026-09-30
23:45:00 and no row is missing. A period in which nothing was measured is still present, as rows
whose values are `NaN`, so the number of rows in a period is the length of that period and never a
count of measurements.

This is the part of a data file that is most often misread when it changes hands. Before you re-
aggregate to hourly or daily values, or compare these data against another dataset, check that
dataset's convention against these three facts.


## 4. What this file contains, and what it does not

**A missing value is `NaN`, and never `0`.** The difference matters most for the signed and the
accumulating variables: for soil heat flux `0` means that no heat crossed the plate, and for
precipitation it means that no rain fell. Neither of them means that nothing was measured. The csv
writes a missing value as an empty field for the same reason.

**Nothing in this file was computed when it was assembled.** Each series is exactly what its own
notebook exported, and this file only joins those series onto one timestamp index. The
corrections, the fits and the evidence behind them stay in those notebooks and on the per-variable
pages of the documentation, and are not repeated here.

**Some series contain modelled values.** These are gap-filled, so some of their values were
produced by a model rather than by an instrument, and the flag column says which of them were:
`SW_IN`, `TA`, `TA_HOM`, `PPFD_IN`, `RH`, `RH_HOM`, `VPD`, `VPD_HOM`, `PREC`, `PREC_HOM`,
`TS_0.05`, `TS_0.1`, `TS_0.15`, `TS_0.2`, `TS_0.3`, `TS_0.5`, `TS_0.6`. The remaining series are
not gap-filled, so a gap in them stays a gap: `PA`, `LW_IN`, `SWC_0.05`, `SWC_0.05_HOM`,
`SWC_0.1`, `SWC_0.1_HOM`, `SWC_0.2`, `SWC_0.2_HOM`, `SWC_0.3`, `SWC_0.3_HOM`, `SWC_0.5`, `G`,
`SW_OUT`, `LW_OUT`.

**A homogenised column is an estimate rather than a measurement.** The instrument behind a series
was in several cases replaced part way through the record, leaving the eras at different levels,
and a homogenised column puts them onto the level of one reference era so that the series can be
read across the change. For `TA_HOM`, `RH_HOM`, `VPD_HOM`, `PREC_HOM`, `SWC_0.05_HOM`,
`SWC_0.1_HOM`, `SWC_0.2_HOM`, `SWC_0.3_HOM` the measured column is in this file as well: use the
homogenised one when your analysis crosses a change and the measured one when it does not.
`TS_0.05`, `TS_0.1`, `TS_0.15`, `TS_0.2`, `TS_0.3`, `TS_0.5`, `TS_0.6`, `G` ship only in
reconciled form, because the individual sensors behind them are separate instruments and stay in
the notebook that screened them.

📊 Overview figures

One figure per series, each under its own heading so it can be found and linked to directly. Four views per figure:

  • Daily mean over the whole record, with a 30-day rolling mean.
  • Fingerprint: the diive HeatmapDateTime, date on the vertical axis against time of day, so the diurnal cycle and any gaps or shifts are visible at a glance.
  • Annual cycle: monthly mean ± 1 SD.
  • Provenance flag, % per year: the stacked composition of the flag codes, or, where a series carries no flag (PA), the distribution of its 30-min values. The bar of 2026 covers January to September and is marked as such.

The daily and annual views of PREC show totals instead of means. The series of 08 to 13 end in December 2025, so their figures stop there.

The measured and homogenised figures of TA, RH, VPD and PREC are worth comparing. The probe changes of January 2016 and June 2026 shift the level of each measured series and are removed in the homogenised one. For PREC, the acquisition break of mid-2018 is plain in the measured series’ daily totals and annual cycle, and gone from the homogenised one.

def overview_figure(series, flag, short, units, flag_labels, agg='mean'):
    """Draw a four-view overview for one variable and return the figure.

    *agg* is 'mean' for state variables and 'sum' for fluxes of mass such as precipitation, where
    a daily or monthly MEAN of 30-min values is not a quantity anyone uses. It changes the two
    time-aggregating panels only; the fingerprint always shows the 30-min values themselves.
    """
    if agg not in ('mean', 'sum'):
        raise ValueError(f"agg must be 'mean' or 'sum', got {agg!r}")
    _is_sum = agg == 'sum'

    fig = plt.figure(figsize=(17, 8.5), dpi=90, layout='constrained')
    gs = gridspec.GridSpec(2, 3, figure=fig, width_ratios=[1.4, 1.4, 1.0])
    fig.suptitle(f"{short}   [{units}]", fontsize=18, fontweight='bold')

    # Daily mean (or total) + 30-day rolling mean
    ax = fig.add_subplot(gs[0, 0:2])
    _d = series.resample('D').sum(min_count=1) if _is_sum else series.resample('D').mean()
    _dunits = 'mm d$^{-1}$' if _is_sum else units
    ax.plot(_d.index, _d.values, lw=0.4, color='#4C72B0', alpha=.55)
    ax.plot(_d.index, _d.rolling(30, center=True).mean().values, lw=1.3, color='#C44E52',
            label='30-day mean')
    ax.set_title('Daily total' if _is_sum else 'Daily mean', fontsize=11)
    ax.set_ylabel(_dunits)
    ax.legend(fontsize=8, loc='upper right')
    ax.grid(alpha=.3)

    # Diurnal fingerprint (diive HeatmapDateTime)
    ax = fig.add_subplot(gs[0:2, 2])
    dv.plotting.HeatmapDateTime(series=series).plot(
        ax=ax, cb_digits_after_comma=0 if not _is_sum else 1,
        format_style=dv.plotting.FormatStyle(title='Fingerprint', title_fontsize=11))

    # Annual cycle: monthly mean +/- SD, or the mean monthly total +/- SD across years
    ax = fig.add_subplot(gs[1, 0])
    if _is_sum:
        _monthly = series.resample('MS').sum(min_count=1)
        _g = _monthly.groupby(_monthly.index.month)
        _title, _ylab = 'Annual cycle (mean monthly total ± SD)', 'mm month$^{-1}$'
    else:
        _g = series.groupby(series.index.month)
        _title, _ylab = 'Annual cycle (monthly mean ± SD)', units
    _m, _sd = _g.mean(), _g.std()
    ax.plot(_m.index, _m.values, color='#C44E52', marker='o', ms=3)
    ax.fill_between(_m.index, (_m - _sd).values, (_m + _sd).values, color='#C44E52', alpha=.18)
    ax.set_title(_title, fontsize=11)
    ax.set_xlabel('month')
    ax.set_ylabel(_ylab)
    ax.set_xticks(range(1, 13))
    ax.grid(alpha=.3)

    # Flag composition per year, or the value distribution where there is no flag
    ax = fig.add_subplot(gs[1, 1])
    if flag is not None:
        comp = flag.groupby([flag.index.year, flag]).size().unstack(fill_value=0)
        comp = comp.div(comp.sum(axis=1), axis=0) * 100
        # A qualitative palette indexed by the code itself, not by its position, so neighbouring
        # codes never share a hue and a code keeps its colour in every year and every series.
        cmap = plt.get_cmap('tab10')
        bottom = np.zeros(len(comp))
        for code in comp.columns:
            _lab = flag_labels.get(int(code), f'flag {int(code)}')
            ax.bar(comp.index, comp[code].values, bottom=bottom, width=.9,
                   color=cmap(int(code) % 10), label=f'{int(code)} {_lab}')
            bottom += comp[code].values
        # A partial year's shares are shares of fewer months, and the bar says so.
        for _y in comp.index[comp.index > LAST_FULL_YEAR]:
            ax.text(_y, 50, f"Jan-{pd.Timestamp(PERIOD_END):%b}", rotation=90, ha='center',
                    va='center', fontsize=7, bbox=dict(fc='white', ec='none', alpha=.75, pad=1))
        ax.set_title('Provenance flag, % per year', fontsize=11)
        ax.set_ylabel('%')
        ax.set_ylim(0, 100)
        # The stacked bars fill the axes from 0 to 100%, so an in-axes legend always
        # covers data. Put it below the panel; constrained layout reserves the space.
        ax.legend(fontsize=8, ncol=3, loc='upper center', bbox_to_anchor=(0.5, -0.13),
                  frameon=False, handlelength=1.3, handletextpad=.5, columnspacing=1.4)
    else:
        ax.hist(series.dropna().values, bins=60, color='#55A868', alpha=.85)
        ax.set_title('Distribution of 30-min values (no flag)', fontsize=11)
        ax.set_xlabel(units)
        ax.set_ylabel('count')
    ax.grid(alpha=.3)
    return fig


PLOTTED = set()  # every series drawn below, checked against PRODUCTS once they are all done


def plot_product(short):
    """Draw the overview figure for the PRODUCTS entry with this short name.

    Each figure gets its own cell and heading below, so that a series can be navigated and
    linked to on its own. The settings still come from PRODUCTS: a cell names a series, it
    does not restate its columns, units or aggregation.
    """
    _entry = next((p for p in PRODUCTS if p[3] == short), None)
    if _entry is None:
        raise KeyError(f"{short!r} is not a short name in PRODUCTS")
    _stem, _vcol, _fcol, _short, _units, _agg = _entry
    # A series can carry several flags. The first is the one whose composition is drawn,
    # and FLAG_LABELS[_short] is that flag's vocabulary, so the two must stay in step.
    _fcols = flagcols(_fcol)
    _flag = merged[_fcols[0]] if _fcols else None
    PLOTTED.add(_short)
    _fig = overview_figure(merged[_vcol], _flag, _short, _units,
                           FLAG_LABELS.get(_short, {}), agg=_agg)

    # Built from this series' own entry, so a series added to PRODUCTS cannot arrive without a
    # caption, and one whose aggregation or flag column changes cannot keep a caption describing
    # what it used to be.
    _daily = 'the daily total and its 30-day mean' if _agg == 'sum' else \
             'the daily mean and its 30-day mean'
    _cycle = 'the mean monthly total' if _agg == 'sum' else 'the monthly mean'
    # Names the flag rather than saying 'the provenance flag': where a series carries two,
    # the panel shows one of them and the caption has to say which.
    _ends_2025 = _entry in NOT_YET_EXTENDED
    _fourth = ((f'the share of each code of {_fcols[0]} per year, ending with {LAST_FULL_YEAR} '
                f'because this product is not yet extended' if _ends_2025 else
                f'the share of each code of {_fcols[0]} per year, the bar of {LAST_FULL_YEAR + 1} '
                f'covering January to {pd.Timestamp(PERIOD_END):%B} only') if _fcols else
               'the distribution of the 30-min values, because this series carries no flag')
    _gaps = MAY_HAVE_GAPS.get(_short)
    figcap(_fig, f"overview of {_vcol} [{_units}], as merged from {_stem}. "
                 f"Top: {_daily}. Right: the 30-min fingerprint, time of day against date. "
                 f"Bottom left: the annual cycle as {_cycle} with its standard deviation across "
                 f"years. Bottom centre: {_fourth}. "
                 + (f"Gaps are expected in this series - {_gaps} - so a blank stripe in the "
                    f"fingerprint is a missing sensor rather than a missing value."
                    if _gaps else
                    "This series is complete over the whole period, so any blank in the "
                    "fingerprint would be a defect."))
    plt.show()


# Negative control: an unknown name must raise instead of quietly drawing nothing, because a

except KeyError:
    pass
else:
    raise AssertionError("plot_product accepted a name that is not in PRODUCTS")
