Download and prepare TA (2004-2026.09)

Modified

27 October 2026

notebook version: 9 (22 Oct 2026)

ℹ️ About this notebook

Downloads screened air temperature TA from InfluxDB, merges the sources, corrects the 2012 logger timestamp shift, gap-fills, and writes the product (2004 to 30 September 2026) to the external data folder. The structure follows notebook 01 (SW_IN), but the gap-filler is the general XGBoostTS instead of SWINGapFillerXGBoost (see Method).

The 47 m record comes from three instruments. On 21 January 2016 the sensor and the acquisition system were replaced together, and the series steps by about 1.3 °C. The step exceeds the warming signal, so any statistic spanning it (a period mean, a trend, a ranking of years) is dominated by the instrument unless the break is handled. The section The 2016 acquisition break documents the break from the data, the logger programs and the fieldbook, and derives the two-term correction used for the homogenised column. Alternatives are scored in TA_HOMOGENIZATION_OPTIONS.ipynb. The CS215 itself changed once, on 16 May 2025: it read abnormally warm in sun from autumn 2024 until the probe was handled that day, and carried no daytime shield excess after it (step 1️⃣1️⃣). On 5 June 2026 a Campbell HygroVUE10 replaced the CS215 under the same field name. It reads cooler in sun, and step 1️⃣2️⃣ brings it onto the level of the normal CS215 against MeteoSwiss Lägern.

Output columns:

  • TA_T1_47_1_gfXG: gap-filled, as measured (°C, complete). Not homogeneous across 21 January 2016, 16 May 2025 or 5 June 2026.
  • FLAG_TA_T1_47_1_ISFILLED: 0 = measured, >0 = filled (codes in FLAG_LEGEND below)
  • TA_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED_gfXG: comparable across the sensor change. A constant raises pre-2016 values onto the post-2016 night level; it removes the zero-point error of the old analog acquisition, which acted day and night. A radiation-dependent term, fitted against the aspirated NABEL sensor on the same tower, removes the extra warming of the post-2016 radiation shield in sun. The CS215 from 16 May 2025, which lacks that extra warming, receives a constant night term instead; the heated CS215 before it (autumn 2024 to 16 May 2025) is corrected as the normal CS215 and stays too warm in sun. The HygroVUE10 era from 5 June 2026 is first brought onto the normal CS215 level by a radiation-dependent term fitted against MeteoSwiss Lägern, then corrected as the CS215 era. Use this column for anything that crosses 2016, May 2025 or June 2026.
  • FLAG_TA_T1_47_1_SOURCE: sensor and acquisition system of each value (codes in FLAG_SOURCE_LEGEND below)

Gap-filling is split at every sensor change, and fill quality differs by period. Five models are fitted: 2004 to the changeover (MP101A, NABEL as a driver), the changeover to the end of 2018 (CS215, with NABEL), 2019 to the CS215 change on 16 May 2025 (without NABEL), from there to the probe change on 5 June 2026, and the HygroVUE10 era to September 2026. No model spans a sensor change, so the homogenisation also holds for modelled records.

InfluxIO needs influxdb-client, which uv sync installs via the diive[db] extra.

📇 Data sources

Series

  • TA_T1_47_1: ETH, 47 m, the product series. mst (2005-2021), diive (2022-2026). A Rotronic MP101A read as analog single-ended voltage until 31 December 2015, a Campbell CS215 read over SDI-12 from 21 January 2016, and a Campbell HygroVUE10 read over SDI-12 from 5 June 2026. The first two are identified from the logger programs in The 2016 acquisition break, the third from the fieldbook in its step 1️⃣2️⃣. The CS215 changed state on 16 May 2025 (step 1️⃣1️⃣).
  • TA_T1_35_1: ETH, 35 m, the profile sensor of the same tower, raw 1-minute data from 2022 (no screened version exists), averaged to 30MIN here. Neither a driver nor part of the product: step 1️⃣1️⃣ uses it to date and attribute the May 2025 change.
  • TA_NABEL_T1_49_1: NABEL, 49 m, same tower, to about 2018. Gap-filling driver, and the reference that resolves the 2016 break, because it spans the break on the same tower.
  • TA_LAE_MS: MeteoSwiss Lägern, 2.5 km, 845 m, 2004-2026, from 10_REFERENCE/MeteoSwiss_LAE_2004-2026.09.ipynb. Gap-filling driver. Confirms independently that the tower sensor, not NABEL, changed in 2016.
  • SW_IN_LAE_MS, WS_LAE_MS: shortwave and wind speed from the same file. Not drivers. The break section bins the residual between the two stations by them, to show that what remains after the homogenisation is radiative.
  • SW_IN_T1_47_1_gfXG: gap-filled SW_IN from notebook 01, which must run first. Gap-filling driver over the whole record, and the input of the radiation-dependent homogenisation term.

The XGBoost gap-filler adds potential radiation and timestamp features.

⏱️ Timestamp convention

The database stores timestamps in UTC as TIMESTAMP_END. On download, InfluxIO shifts them by TIMEZONE_OFFSET_TO_UTC_HOURS, so the data arrive in local time (UTC + offset), still as TIMESTAMP_END. TimestampSanitizer(output_middle_timestamp=True) then converts to TIMESTAMP_MID, which the rest of this notebook uses. START and STOP are in the same timezone.

    """Print a table caption, above the table it belongs to."""

def figcap(fig, text, width=125):
    """Place a caption under a figure. Call after the last axes of that figure is drawn.

    A figure with a layout engine makes room for a caption itself. The heatmaps diive builds have
    no engine, and a caption placed in one lands on top of the tick labels; for those the figure
    is extended downwards and every axes moved up by the same amount, so the drawing keeps its
    size and position and the caption gets a free strip below it.
    """
    width = min(width, max(40, int(14.5 * fig.get_size_inches()[0])))
    caption = textwrap.fill(f"Figure - {text}", width)
    # plt.tight_layout() leaves a PlaceHolderLayoutEngine that never runs again, so a caption given
    # to supxlabel there lands on the tick labels. Those figures take the manual branch below.
    _engine = fig.get_layout_engine()
    if _engine is not None and type(_engine).__name__ != 'PlaceHolderLayoutEngine':
        fig.supxlabel(caption, fontsize=9, ha='left', x=0.005)
        return
    lines = len(caption.splitlines())
    extra = 0.17 * lines + 0.12          # inches; one caption line is about 0.17 in at fontsize 9
    width_in, height_in = fig.get_size_inches()
    fig.set_size_inches(width_in, height_in + extra)
    keep = height_in / (height_in + extra)
    offset = extra / (height_in + extra)
    for ax in fig.axes:
        pos = ax.get_position()
        ax.set_position([pos.x0, offset + pos.y0 * keep, pos.width, pos.height * keep])
    fig.text(0.005, 0.01, caption, fontsize=9, ha='left', va='bottom')

🔎 The 2016 acquisition break

On 21 January 2016 the 47 m sensor and the acquisition system were replaced together, and the record steps by about 1.3 °C, the same order as the warming signal. Its effect on the period means and the trend is computed in What the break does to the numbers this product is used for below.

This section establishes, from data or from files on disk:

  1. that a step exists, and when (difference against MeteoSwiss);
  2. that this notebook does not produce it (the corrections above and the mst/diive splice);
  3. which sensor moved (NABEL at 49 m spans the break on the same tower);
  4. which side of the break is correct (at night, where a radiation-shield error cannot contribute);
  5. what caused it (the two logger programs and the fieldbook);
  6. that the error is a constant, which justifies the constant correction of step 8️⃣. The rejected alternatives follow it.

Step 9️⃣ scores the result against MeteoSwiss, which entered none of the derivation: the step is gone at night and remains in daylight. Step 🔟 removes the daylight part by fitting each sensor era’s radiation-shield response against the aspirated NABEL sensor and standardising the later era onto the earlier one. The exported homogenised column carries both terms. Step 1️⃣1️⃣ documents the change of the CS215 on 16 May 2025, after which it no longer carries the shield excess, and corrects that era by a night term. Step 1️⃣2️⃣ brings the HygroVUE10 that replaced the CS215 on 5 June 2026 onto the level of the normal CS215, so both terms then apply to it as well.

MeteoSwiss Lägern is 2.5 km from the tower and is operated by a different institution, so the difference between the two stations removes the weather and leaves the instruments. A constant offset is expected from the elevation difference; what matters is whether that offset stays constant.

# --- Fieldbook and logger-script redaction ----------------------------------------------------
# Both the GIN export and the logger programs name people. This notebook renders to a public page,
# so every string taken out of either passes through redact_people() at the single point where it
# is flattened. Same map and same rules as notebook 08; it lives beside the export, OUTSIDE this
# repository, so the repo never carries a real name.
REDACT_MAP = Path(FIELDBOOK).with_name('redact_names.csv')
ALLOW_LIST = Path(FIELDBOOK).with_name('redact_allow.csv')
def unmapped_report_path(label: str) -> Path:
    """Where the unmapped-name candidates of one audit are written.

    Per label, because this notebook audits two sources: a single shared file would let the
    second audit overwrite the first one's findings.
    """
    _slug = re.sub(r'[^a-z0-9]+', '_', label.lower()).strip('_')
    return Path(FIELDBOOK).with_name(f'redact_unmapped_{_slug}.txt')


def load_redactions(path=None) -> dict:
    """Load the name -> pseudonym map.

    Raises if the map is missing. That is the point: carrying on with redaction quietly disabled
    prints real names into a published page and looks exactly like success.
    """
    path = REDACT_MAP if path is None else Path(path)
    if not path.is_file():
        raise FileNotFoundError(
            f"the redaction map is missing: {path}. Without it the names these files record would "
            f"print unredacted. Restore the file rather than skipping this.")
    tab = pd.read_csv(path, encoding='utf-8')
    missing = {'name', 'pseudonym'}.difference(tab.columns)
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"{path}: missing column(s) {sorted(missing)}.")
    pairs = {str(n).strip(): str(p).strip()
             for n, p in zip(tab['name'], tab['pseudonym']) if str(n).strip()}
    if not pairs:
        raise ValueError(f"{path}: holds no usable name -> pseudonym pairs.")
    return pairs


REDACTIONS = load_redactions()
# Longest surface form first, so a bare surname cannot corrupt a double-barrelled one that starts
# with it. Lookarounds rather than \b, because some forms end in an initial.
_REDACT_RX = re.compile(r'(?<!\w)(' + '|'.join(re.escape(n) for n in
                                               sorted(REDACTIONS, key=len, reverse=True))
                        + r')(?!\w)')


def redact_people(s) -> str:
    """Replace every mapped name with its stable pseudonym."""
    return _REDACT_RX.sub(lambda m: REDACTIONS[m.group(1)], str(s))


def strip_html(text) -> str:
    """Flatten one GIN fieldbook entry to plain text, with the people it names redacted."""
    flat = html.unescape(re.sub(r'<[^>]+>', ' ', str(text)))
    return redact_people(re.sub(r'\s+', ' ', flat).strip())


_NAME_TOKEN = r"[A-ZÄÖÜ][\wÄÖÜäöüß'-]{1,20}"
_NAME_UNIT = _NAME_TOKEN + r'(?:\s+' + _NAME_TOKEN + r'){0,2}'
_NAME_SHAPES = (
    re.compile(r'\((' + _NAME_UNIT + r'(?:\s*[,+&]\s*' + _NAME_UNIT + r')*)\s*'
               r'(?:\d{1,2}\.\d{1,2}\.\d{4})?\)'),
    re.compile(r'(?:with|by|durch|von|gemaess)\s+(' + _NAME_TOKEN + r'(?:\s+' + _NAME_TOKEN + r')?)'),
)


def audit_unmapped_names(texts, label='fieldbook', write_report: bool = True) -> int:
    """Raise if any name-shaped token survived redaction.

    A newer export can name someone the map has never seen. Candidates are never printed - an
    exception message is stored in a notebook like any other output - so they go to a file beside
    the map and only the count is shown.
    """
    allow = set(pd.read_csv(ALLOW_LIST, encoding='utf-8')['term'].astype(str).str.strip())
    found = {}
    for _t in texts:
        for _rx in _NAME_SHAPES:
            for _m in _rx.finditer(str(_t)):
                for _tok in re.findall(_NAME_TOKEN, _m.group(1)):
                    if _tok.isupper() or any(ch.isdigit() for ch in _tok) or _tok in allow:
                        continue
                    found[_tok] = found.get(_tok, 0) + 1
    if found:
        _report = unmapped_report_path(label)
        if write_report:
            _report.write_text(
                '\n'.join(f'{n}\t{t}' for t, n in sorted(found.items(), key=lambda kv: -kv[1])),
                encoding='utf-8')
        raise ValueError(
            f"{label}: {len(found)} name-shaped token(s) survived redaction. They are deliberately "
            f"NOT shown here - see {_report}. Add the people to redact_names.csv and the "
            f"technical terms to redact_allow.csv, then re-run.")
    print(f"  {label}: no unmapped name-shaped tokens ({len(allow)} allowlisted terms)")
    return 0


def program_excerpt(path, pattern: str, before: int = 6, after: int = 4,
                    expect: int = 1) -> str:

7️⃣ The error is a constant, so a constant corrects it

A single offset is legitimate only if the error is a single offset. With a voltage scaled by Mult 0.1, a zero-point error (a constant voltage at the input) gives a bias that is constant in °C, and a span or gain error gives one that grows with temperature. The night bias against NABEL is therefore binned by air temperature below: a flat profile indicates a zero-point error, and a sloped one would rule out a constant correction.

# Two helpers for this step. Both average over a selection, and both would report a mean over a
# handful of half-hours in the same column as a mean over three years, so both refuse to.
def residual_step(residual, window, pre_years, post_years, min_records=500):
    """Mean of `residual` over `pre_years` and over `post_years`, within `window`.

    `residual` is a tower-minus-reference series and `window` a boolean mask on the same index,
    selecting night, day or all hours. The two are intersected, so a window that misses a period
    is possible and has to be loud rather than plausible.
    """
    _pre = window & residual.index.year.isin(list(pre_years))
    _post = window & residual.index.year.isin(list(post_years))
    if _pre.sum() < min_records or _post.sum() < min_records:
        raise ValueError(f"too few records to compare: {_pre.sum()} in {list(pre_years)}, "
                         f"{_post.sum()} in {list(post_years)}, need {min_records} in each")
    return _pre.sum() + _post.sum(), residual[_pre].mean(), residual[_post].mean()


def binned_residual(frame, column, bins, min_records=150):
    """Mean residual and record count per bin of `column`, over the rows of `frame`.

    An empty or nearly empty `frame` produces a column of nan means and zero counts, which plots
    as an absent line rather than as an error, so the bin occupancy is checked here instead.
    """
    _f = frame.dropna(subset=[column, 'residual'])
    _g = _f.groupby(pd.cut(_f[column], bins), observed=False)['residual']
    _mean, _count = _g.mean(), _g.count()
    if _count.min() < min_records:
        raise ValueError(f"bin {_count.idxmin()} of {column} holds {_count.min()} records, "
                         f"fewer than the {min_records} needed to average")
    return _mean, _count

🔟 Removing what a constant cannot: the shield term

NABEL at 49 m is aspirated, so its own radiative error is negligible, and it overlaps eleven years of the MP101A and three of the CS215. The difference 47 m − NABEL as a function of the tower’s incoming shortwave is therefore the radiation-shield response of the sensor in place.

Removing the radiation-dependent part of the difference between the two responses puts the CS215 era onto the MP101A era’s shield behaviour, and the constant of step 8️⃣ keeps one night level. The exported column thus has the night level of the CS215 era and the daytime behaviour of the MP101A era. The MP101A’s own shield error remains in both eras, so the record is self-consistent across the sensor change but not corrected to an aspirated measurement.

# The correction. Two terms acting on different eras, and neither touches the other's records. Two
# more are derived later and act on their own eras only: the night term of the CS215 after its
# change of May 2025 (step 11), and the transfer of the HygroVUE10 that replaced it in June 2026
# (step 12). Until those steps have run, C6_TERMS and HV_TERMS are empty and homogenize() refuses
# the records they describe rather than passing them through on the correction of another era.
def radiative_excess(sw):
    """The radiation-dependent part of the CS215-minus-MP101A difference, in degC.

    Zero where there is no sun, by construction: the zero-radiation part of that difference is the
    zero-point error of the analog chain, which OFFSET already removes. Subtracting it here as well
    would correct the night twice.
    """
    _x = shield_basis(np.clip(np.atleast_1d(sw), 0.0, SHIELD_SW_MAX))
    _x0 = shield_basis(np.zeros(len(np.atleast_1d(sw))))
    return (_x - _x0) @ (SHIELD['CS215'] - SHIELD['MP101A'])


C6_TERMS = None   # the night term of the CS215 after the May 2025 change, set in step 11
HV_TERMS = None   # the HygroVUE10 transfer, set in step 12


def hv_excess(sw, terms=None):
    """The radiation-dependent part of the HygroVUE10-minus-CS215 difference, in degC.

    Built like radiative_excess(): the same basis, the driver clipped to the radiation range both
    samples were fitted on, and zero where there is no sun by construction. `terms` defaults to
    the fit of step 12; the month hold-out there passes its own refits.
    """
    _t = HV_TERMS if terms is None else terms
    if _t is None:
        raise RuntimeError("the HygroVUE10 terms are derived in step 12, which has not run yet")
    _sw = np.atleast_1d(np.asarray(sw, dtype=float))
    _x = shield_basis(np.clip(_sw, 0.0, _t['sw_max']))
    _x0 = shield_basis(np.zeros(len(_sw)))
    return (_x - _x0) @ (_t['beta_hv'] - _t['beta_cs'])


def homogenize(values, index, sw):
    """The homogenised series: one night level and one daytime shield behaviour for all eras.

    By time, so a modelled record is corrected as the period that modelled it:
    - before BREAK (MP101A, the changeover outage): the constant;
    - BREAK to RESET_START (CS215 codes 0 and 7, and the handling record): the shield term;
    - RESET_START to HV_START (CS215 code 6, and the probe swap record): the constant night term of
      step 11 only, since the CS215 no longer carries the shield excess the shield term removes;
    - from HV_START (HygroVUE10): hv_excess and the night term of step 12, then the shield term.
    """
    _index = pd.DatetimeIndex(index)
    _sw = np.atleast_1d(np.asarray(sw, dtype=float))
    _c6 = np.asarray((_index >= RESET_START) & (_index < HV_START))
    _hv = np.asarray(_index >= HV_START)
    _out = np.asarray(values, dtype=float) + np.where(_index < BREAK, OFFSET, 0.0)
    _out = _out - np.where(np.asarray(_index >= BREAK) & ~_c6, radiative_excess(_sw), 0.0)
    if _c6.any():
        if C6_TERMS is None:
            raise RuntimeError(f"{int(_c6.sum())} records of the CS215 after May 2025 passed to "
                               f"homogenize() before step 11 has derived their term")
        _out = _out - np.where(_c6, C6_TERMS['night'], 0.0)
    if _hv.any():
        if HV_TERMS is None:
            raise RuntimeError(f"{int(_hv.sum())} HygroVUE10 records passed to homogenize() before "
                               f"step 12 has derived their terms")
        _out = _out - np.where(_hv, hv_excess(_sw) + HV_TERMS['night'], 0.0)
    return _out

# The onset of code 7. The excess grows over weeks after the tubing entry rather than stepping, and
# sunny half-hours are scarce in autumn, so it is dated by a rule on a smoothed statistic:
#   the excess on a day is TA 47 m - TA 35 m over the sunlit records (tower SW_IN > EXCESS_SW_MIN)
#   of the EXCESS_WINDOW days centred on it, minus the same over the night records, where the
#   window holds at least EXCESS_MIN_RECORDS sunlit records. The onset is the first day on or after
#   TUBING_DATE on which the excess exceeds the largest value it reaches before TUBING_DATE.
# Day minus night removes any change in the level of the pair; only the daytime excess remains.
EXCESS_SW_MIN = 200          # W m-2
EXCESS_WINDOW = 31           # days
EXCESS_MIN_RECORDS = 50      # sunlit half-hours in the window


def excess_statistic(frame):
    """The centred-window daytime excess of TA 47 m - TA 35 m over its night level, per day."""
    _d = frame['TA 47 m - TA 35 m']
    _day = frame.index.normalize()
    _sun = (frame['sw'] > EXCESS_SW_MIN) & _d.notna()
    _ngt = frame['night'] & _d.notna()
    _days = pd.date_range(_day.min(), _day.max(), freq='D')

    def _roll(s):
        return s.reindex(_days).fillna(0).rolling(EXCESS_WINDOW, center=True,
                                                   min_periods=EXCESS_WINDOW).sum()
    _n_sun = _roll(_sun.groupby(_day).sum())
    _stat = (_roll(_d.where(_sun).groupby(_day).sum()) / _n_sun
             - _roll(_d.where(_ngt).groupby(_day).sum()) / _roll(_ngt.groupby(_day).sum()))
    return _stat.where(_n_sun >= EXCESS_MIN_RECORDS)

On 5 June 2026 a Campbell HygroVUE10 (SN E6730, SDI-12) replaced the CS215 at 47 m. The field name TA_T1_47_1 and its database tags did not change. The change is dated by three independent observations:

  • The raw record. The 1-minute TA_T1_47_1 has a gap from 11:10 to 11:31 (local time, TIMESTAMP_END) that day. The first HygroVUE10 half-hour is therefore the one ending 12:00 (HV_START), and the half-hour 11:00 to 11:30 is a partial or mixed record (SWAP_RECORD, removed in Corrections, source code 5).
  • The fieldbook. It adds the HygroVUE10 at CH-LAE_T1_47 on 5 June, and on 11 June changes its SDI-12 configuration ID together with the logger program. It has no entry for the removal of the CS215, which GIN never registered at that location. The cell below asserts both dates.
  • The data. The residual against MeteoSwiss Lägern changes level from that day, most in sun (tables below).

Reference level. The HygroVUE10 era is brought onto the level of the normal CS215, and the shield term of step 🔟 is then applied to it as to that era, so all eras end on the same reference. The measured column keeps the HygroVUE10 values as measured.

Method. NABEL ended in 2018, so Lägern is the only reference that spans the change, and the transfer is fitted against it on two season-matched samples: the HygroVUE10 from 12 June to 30 September 2026 (5 to 11 June, before the configuration change, are transitional and enter no fit), and the normal CS215 (code 0) on the same calendar dates of 2022 to 2024. 2025 is not used: the CS215 was heated until 16 May that year and changed state after it (step 1️⃣1️⃣).

  • Radiation term. The residual (tower minus Lägern) of each sample is fitted against tower SW_IN with shield_basis, the basis of the shield term. hv_excess is the difference of the two curves with its zero-radiation part removed, so it is zero in the dark, and the driver is clipped to the range both samples cover.
  • Night term. A constant equal to the night difference between the samples. The probe change is established (fieldbook, raw-data gap, radiation-dependent step), so the night level is corrected as well. The year-to-year scatter of the CS215 night level (largest departure of one CS215 year from the mean of the other two) is reported as its uncertainty.
  • Scoring. With no second reference to score against, each month of 2026 is left out in turn, the transfer is refitted on the rest, and the month is predicted.

def fit_hv_transfer(frame):
    """Least-squares fit of the tower-minus-Laegern residual in tower SW_IN, on shield_basis.

    Raises on a sample too small to fit or too dark to constrain the radiation curve: a fit on a
    few days returns coefficients as confidently as one on a summer.
    """
    _sunny = int((frame['sw'] > 500).sum())
    if len(frame) < HV_MIN_RECORDS or _sunny < HV_MIN_SUNNY:
        raise ValueError(f"{len(frame)} records ({_sunny} above 500 W m-2), need {HV_MIN_RECORDS} "
                         f"({HV_MIN_SUNNY}) to fit a radiation response")
    _beta, *_ = np.linalg.lstsq(shield_basis(frame['sw']), frame['residual'].to_numpy(), rcond=None)
    return _beta


def hv_terms(frame):

    The night term is the night difference between the samples, applied as a constant: the probe
    change is established, so its night level is corrected as well as its radiation response. The
    CS215 year-to-year scatter of the same quantity (the largest departure of one CS215 year's night
    mean from the mean of the other years) is returned as the uncertainty of that term. The CS215
    night level is the mean of its yearly night means, so each year weighs the same as in the
    scatter.

Limitations of the HygroVUE10 term

  • One summer. The transfer is fitted on 12 June to 30 September 2026 and applied, as fitted, to every HygroVUE10 record, including 5 to 11 June. Its behaviour in winter, under snow or rime and at low sun is untested.
  • One reference. Lägern is 2.5 km away and about 100 m higher, and both the fit and the score use it. The month hold-out tests whether the transfer carries to data it was not fitted on, not whether it agrees with an independent instrument.
  • The CS215 level is a mean of three summers that differ among themselves at night by an amount comparable to the night term itself, so the night term is uncertain by that amount (printed above). The hold-out table shows how much it changes when one month is left out.
  • The reference level is unchanged. Like the CS215 era, the HygroVUE10 era ends on the shield behaviour of the MP101A, so the limitations of step 🔟 apply to it as well.

What the correction does not fix

The first item is measured in steps 9️⃣ and 🔟 against MeteoSwiss, which entered neither term. The three-year window and, for the constant, the full eras give the same result.

  • The shield error the two sensors share remains. Both eras carry the MP101A’s own error and read warm in sun and light wind throughout the record. Daily maxima and diurnal ranges are biased warm in every year, by a comparable amount on both sides of 2016. The step that survives is the shield row of the step table in step 🔟. Removing the shared error would require the aspirated scale, rejected above.
  • The radiative term depends on a gap-filled driver (SW_IN_T1_47_1_gfXG). Refitting on measured radiation alone changes it by hundredths of a degree, because the driver is almost complete over the CS215 era.
  • The records reconstructed from NABEL carry too little scatter. As values on a fitted line, those 1,530 records sit about 0.05 °C from NABEL, where a real measurement sits about 0.25 °C from it. Their means are sound; variance, extremes and other spread statistics over the periods they cover are understated. Most fall in January 2004; the export section gives the breakdown by month.
  • 2004 has no measurement before 20 September. 72 % of that year is modelled, and January is reconstructed from NABEL rather than gap-filled, because MeteoSwiss begins on 1 February 2004. It carries source code 3 and is the least constrained year.
  • The mechanism of the zero-point error is inferred. The class of error is established; whether the 11 mV came from the probe’s zero trim or from a ground offset in the cabling is not.
  • The heated CS215 (code 7) stays too warm in sun. It is corrected as the normal CS215 only; step 1️⃣1️⃣ measures what remains. The night term of the CS215 after May 2025 (code 6) is smaller than its uncertainty. Both are listed at the end of step 1️⃣1️⃣.
  • The HygroVUE10 term rests on one summer and one reference. Its limitations are listed at the end of step 1️⃣2️⃣.

🟪 Prepare dataframes

Split into five periods, gap-filled separately. The four boundaries have different causes:

  • The sensor change on 21 January 2016. A model trained across this 1.3 °C discontinuity produces values on neither era’s level. The boundary is what makes the homogenisation valid for filled records.
  • The end of NABEL, a change of drivers, with the same instrument.
  • The CS215 change on 16 May 2025 (step 1️⃣1️⃣) and the probe change on 5 June 2026 (CS215 to HygroVUE10, step 1️⃣2️⃣), for the same reason as the first. The probe half-hour before each belongs to the period before it.

The January 2016 outage belongs to the first period, so the offset region and P1 cover the same span. The measured column then steps only at the sensor changes, and the homogenised column is continuous across them. The first two cut-offs are taken from the data; the other two are settings, checked against the data and the fieldbook in steps 1️⃣1️⃣ and 1️⃣2️⃣.

🟥 Gap-filling

Method

SWINGapFillerXGBoost (notebook 01) is specific to radiation: it zeroes nighttime gaps and negative predictions, removes a nighttime offset and interpolates the clearness index. None of this is valid for temperature; it would write 0 °C into every nighttime and frost gap. This notebook uses the general XGBoostTS with an explicit FeatureEngineer (what SWINGapFillerXGBoost wraps internally) and otherwise follows 01: drivers plus timestamp features, SHAP-based feature reduction, and interpolation of short gaps.

  • features_lag=[]. The model predicts only records with every feature present, so lagging a gappy driver would send more records to the weaker timestamp-only fallback.
  • SW_IN_POT is excluded from the rolling and EMA stages. It follows from the timestamp, so smoothed versions add features without adding information.
  • shap_max_rows=10_000. SHAP is the slowest step, computed four times per call (train, reduce, retrain, fill). Mean importances converge well before the full record, so rows are capped as in SWINGapFillerXGBoost. Only the importances and the feature reduction use the sample, which is reproducible through random_state; predictions and scores do not.
  • Hyperparameters are set explicitly, to the values SWINGapFillerXGBoost uses, because XGBoostTS ships no tuned defaults.

Short gaps (up to 1 h) are then filled by linear interpolation and flagged 4. The target is excluded from its own features, so the model cannot see a gap’s measured neighbours. Temperature is continuous across midnight, so the interpolation is not restricted to single days.

SHAP_MAX_ROWS = 10_000  # rows used for SHAP importances, see the note above


def short_gap_mask(series: pd.Series, limit: int) -> pd.Series:
    """True where `series` is missing AND its whole gap is at most `limit` records long.

    Whole runs are selected explicitly. Using pandas' `interpolate(limit=)` instead would fill
    the first `limit` records of a long gap as well, which is not wanted: those values would sit
    on a straight line spanning the entire gap.
    """
    missing = series.isna()
    run_length = missing.groupby((~missing).cumsum()).transform('sum')
    return missing & (run_length <= limit)


def gapfill_ta(target: pd.Series,
               drivers: pd.DataFrame,
               interpolate_limit: int = 2,
               reduce_features: bool = True,
               verbose: int = 1):
    """Gap-fill a temperature series with XGBoost, then interpolate the remaining short gaps.

    Returns (model, gapfilled, flag). `gapfilled` carries the `_gfXG` name, `flag` follows
    FLAG_LEGEND.
    """
    input_df = pd.concat([target, drivers], axis=1)

    engineer = dv.gapfilling.FeatureEngineer(
        target_col=target.name,
        features_lag=[],  # see the note above
        features_rolling=[4, 8, 24, 48],  # 2h, 4h, 12h, 24h on 30MIN data
        features_rolling_stats=['median'],
        features_rolling_exclude_cols=['SW_IN_POT'],  # deterministic, see the note above
        features_ema=[6, 24],  # 3h, 12h
        features_ema_exclude_cols=['SW_IN_POT'],
        vectorize_timestamps=True,
        add_continuous_record_number=True,
        verbose=0,
    )
    features_df = engineer.fit_transform(input_df)
    print(f"Features: {features_df.shape[1] - 1} over {len(features_df):,} records")

    model = dv.gapfilling.XGBoostTS(
        input_df=features_df,
        target_col=target.name,
        verbose=verbose,
        shap_max_rows=SHAP_MAX_ROWS,
        n_estimators=3000,
        max_depth=6,
        early_stopping_rounds=20,
        random_state=42,
        n_jobs=-1,
    )
    model.trainmodel(showplot_scores=False, showplot_importance=False)
    if reduce_features:
        model.reduce_features()
        model.trainmodel(showplot_scores=False, showplot_importance=False)
    model.fillgaps(showplot_scores=False, showplot_importance=False)

    gapfilled = model.results.gapfilled.copy()
    flag = model.results.flag.copy()

    if interpolate_limit:
        short = short_gap_mask(target, interpolate_limit)
        interp = target.interpolate(method='time', limit_area='inside')
        fill_locs = short & interp.notna()
        gapfilled[fill_locs] = interp[fill_locs]
        flag[fill_locs] = 4
        print(f"Short-gap interpolation: {int(fill_locs.sum())} records")

    gapfilled = gapfilled.rename(f"{target.name}_gfXG")
    flag = flag.rename(f"FLAG_{target.name}_ISFILLED")
    return model, gapfilled, flag

🩹 Records the gap-filler could only fill from the clock

XGBoost predicts only where every feature is present. Otherwise the record falls to the timestamp-only fallback (ISFILLED == 2), which follows the season but not the weather and writes one prediction per hour to both half-hours.

Most such records are in January 2004 (table below), before MeteoSwiss Lägern begins on 1 February 2004. NABEL, two metres above the target on the same tower, is complete that month. These records are therefore reconstructed from NABEL with a straight line (slope and offset) fitted on the measured records of the same sensor era, and re-coded ISFILLED == 5. Records where NABEL is missing as well keep code 2. The cell assigns values rather than adjusting them, so re-running it changes nothing.

Every modelled record sits on the era it belongs to, and varies like it

The offset is added to every pre-break record, which is valid only if modelled values share the measured era’s level. Two night-time tests against NABEL:

Level, per year and per fill method. A yearly average can hide opposite errors that cancel: an earlier version passed a per-year check while holding 11,150 records 1.1 °C too cold and 1,495 records 5.5 °C too warm.

Spread, the stronger test. A timestamp-only climatology can have the right mean, but it scatters against NABEL several degrees more widely than a measurement.

Neither test is evidence for the records reconstructed from NABEL. Fitted to NABEL, they match its level and scatter less than a measurement. They rest on the slope guard of the fit and on the held-out January test above.

📘 Note for downstream users

Which column to use.

  • Within one sensor era: TA_T1_47_1_gfXG, the values the instrument reported.
  • Across 21 January 2016, 16 May 2025 or 5 June 2026 (period means, trends, rankings of years, anomalies against a multi-year reference): TA_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED_gfXG. The measured column steps by about 1.3 °C at the first date for instrumental reasons, more than the climate signal over this record; it reads too warm in sun from autumn 2024 to 16 May 2025 and without the usual shield excess after it; and its HygroVUE10 era reads cooler in sun than the CS215.
  • The homogenised column differs from the measured one in every era: by a constant before 2016; in the CS215 era (codes 0 and 7) by a radiation-dependent amount that is zero at night and largest in strong sun; in the CS215 after May 2025 (code 6) by the constant night term of step 1️⃣1️⃣; and in the HygroVUE10 era by the radiation-dependent amount and the HygroVUE10 transfer of step 1️⃣2️⃣, which raises it in sun, plus the constant night term of step 1️⃣2️⃣. The difference between the two columns is therefore not a constant.

Which flag to filter on.

  • FLAG_TA_T1_47_1_ISFILLED == 0 selects measured records; all others are modelled. Code 5 marks records reconstructed from NABEL on the same tower, code 2 the few filled from the clock only.
  • FLAG_TA_T1_47_1_SOURCE: 1 Rotronic MP101A era, 0 Campbell CS215 era, 7 the heated CS215 (autumn 2024 to 16 May 2025), 6 the CS215 after its change of 16 May 2025, 4 Campbell HygroVUE10 era from 5 June 2026, 2 the 988 records of the January 2016 outage (neither era), 3 the months before the tower record begins in September 2004, 5 the probe handling and probe swap half-hours. Codes 2, 3 and 5 are everything no instrument stands behind; drop them where provenance must be certain. Drop code 7 for daytime statistics (daily maxima, diurnal ranges): it stays too warm in sun after homogenisation.

Both value columns share both flags: the homogenisation changes values, not provenance.

Known limitations. The homogenised column puts the later era onto the earlier era’s radiation shield, so the shield error both sensors share remains: both read warm in sun and light wind relative to an aspirated measurement. Daily maxima and diurnal ranges are biased warm, by a comparable amount on both sides of 2016. The residual step is measured on every run in What the correction does not fix above. That section also details two limitations the homogenisation does not address: records reconstructed from NABEL carry too little scatter, so spread statistics over their periods are understated, and 2004 has no measurement before 20 September. Code 7 is under-corrected in sun, and the night term of code 6 is smaller than its uncertainty (step 1️⃣1️⃣). The HygroVUE10 transfer is fitted on one summer against one reference and applied as fitted to the whole HygroVUE10 era (step 1️⃣2️⃣). The record ends on 30 September 2026, so the 2026 annual mean covers January to September only.

The evidence is re-derived on every run in The 2016 acquisition break, including its steps 1️⃣1️⃣ and 1️⃣2️⃣ for the May 2025 change and the June 2026 probe change.
