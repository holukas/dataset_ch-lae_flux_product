ℹ️ About this notebook

Downloads screened relative humidity RH from InfluxDB, merges the sources, corrects the August 2012 clock shift, removes the three damaged 2012 periods and the two probe half-hours of 2025 and 2026, applies the RH > 100% offset correction, identifies the instrument behind the column and the effect of its probe changes, fills every gap from NABEL and then MeteoSwiss Lägern, and writes the product to the external data folder. Up to the corrections it follows notebooks 01 (SW_IN), 02 (TA) and 03 (PPFD_IN). It reads 02’s product for the vapour pressure of steps 1️⃣1️⃣ and 1️⃣2️⃣, so 02 runs before this notebook.

ImportantThe measured series is not homogeneous across 21 January 2016 or 5 June 2026

RH_T1_47_1 comes from the same probe as TA_T1_47_1: a Rotronic MP101A (analog) until 21 January 2016, a Campbell CS215 (SDI-12) until 5 June 2026, then a Campbell HygroVUE10 (SDI-12). The 2016 change moved TA by about 1.3 °C (notebook 02) and RH by +2.1 to +3.3 percentage points, +3.9 to +4.1 away from the 100 % ceiling, a step attributed to the tower probe in the section Which sensor this is, and what the hardware change did to it. The 2026 change makes the series drier against MeteoSwiss Lägern, by an amount that grows with the humidity. In between, the CS215 heated abnormally in sun from 22 October 2024 (source code 7) and lost its daytime excess when it was handled on 16 May 2025 (code 6); in RH both are effects of the probe temperature (step 1️⃣1️⃣).

RH_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED puts the other probes on the CS215 level: the MP101A era is raised by a humidity-dependent transfer fitted against NABEL (same tower, 49 m) on the three years before the 2016 break and the CS215 record to July 2018, and scored against MeteoSwiss Lägern (step 🔟); the HygroVUE10 era is transferred in vapour pressure and converted back with 02’s homogenised temperature (step 1️⃣2️⃣). The CS215 is unchanged in all its states, codes 6 and 7 included. RH_T1_47_1 keeps every era as measured.

The 2016 correction depends on the reference. Fitted against Lägern instead of NABEL, it would raise the MP101A era by several tenths of a percentage point more on average and by up to about 2.4 percentage points more just below saturation (step 🔟). That difference is the uncertainty of the homogenised column across 2016.

The homogenised column removes the steps, not the drift or the probe heating. Against Lägern the MP101A era drifts downwards and the CS215 era upwards, each by a few tenths of a percentage point a year (computed in step 🔟); neither drift is corrected, so a multi-year trend carries it. Codes 7 and 6 keep their sun-dependent RH errors, and from June 2026 the homogenised RH refers to 02’s homogenised temperature, so in sun it lies above what a CS215 would read.

ImportantWhat this notebook does and does not do

Gaps are reconstructed from another measurement of the same quantity, offset-corrected month by month within the sensor era; nothing is modelled. NABEL at 49 m (same tower, 2004-2018) fills first (flag 3), MeteoSwiss Lägern (2.5 km, 2004-2026) the rest, chiefly after 2018 (flag 4, larger uncertainty). The exported series is complete.

  • RH_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED: the same series on the CS215 level (%): records before 21 January 2016 raised by the 2016 transfer, records from 5 June 2026 by the vapour pressure transfer relative to 02’s homogenised temperature, the CS215 (codes 0, 6, 7) and the probe half-hours unchanged; drift within each era and the probe heating of codes 6 and 7 are not corrected

def figcap(fig, text, width=125):
    """Place a caption under a figure. Call after the last axes of that figure is drawn."""
    width = min(width, max(40, int(14.5 * fig.get_size_inches()[0])))
    caption = textwrap.fill(f"Figure - {text}", width)
    # An engine that still lays the figure out at draw time can make room for a caption itself.
    # plt.tight_layout() does NOT leave one: it executes once and leaves a PlaceHolderLayoutEngine
    # that never runs again, so a caption handed to supxlabel there lands on top of the tick

    _engine = fig.get_layout_engine()
    if _engine is not None and type(_engine).__name__ != 'PlaceHolderLayoutEngine':
        fig.supxlabel(caption, fontsize=9, ha='left', x=0.005)

    lines = len(caption.splitlines())
    extra = 0.17 * lines + 0.12          # inches; one caption line is about 0.17 in at fontsize 9
    width_in, height_in = fig.get_size_inches()
    fig.set_size_inches(width_in, height_in + extra)
    keep = height_in / (height_in + extra)
    offset = extra / (height_in + extra)
    for ax in fig.axes:
        pos = ax.get_position()
        ax.set_position([pos.x0, offset + pos.y0 * keep, pos.width, pos.height * keep])
    fig.text(0.005, 0.01, caption, fontsize=9, ha='left', va='bottom')

Helper functions

The shared helpers: the RH > 100% correction, the offset transfer used by both reconstruction passes, and two plotting helpers. The plot helpers of 01 and 03 split a series by a gap-filling flag; these split by provenance. Each builds its own caption from the data it is given.

def remove_rh_offset_by_year(series: pd.Series, showplot: bool = False):
    """Apply diive's `RH > 100%` offset correction within each year that contains a value > 100%.

    The library function derives a daily offset from the records above 100% and interpolates and
    ffill/bfills it over the whole index. One call on the full record would also shift years that
    never exceed 100%: a flat +0.71 % over 2004-2006 (bfilled from 2007) and a flat +0.133 % from
    2016 on (ffilled from 2015). A sensor reading 102 at a true 100 is biased over its whole range,
    so the offset is subtracted from every record of an exceedance year; years without evidence of
    drift come through bit-identical.

    A year is also split at every date of PROBE_STATE_CHANGES (the CS215 changes of 2024-2025 and
    the June 2026 probe change), and each part is corrected only if it holds a value > 100% itself:
    an offset derived from one probe state is never subtracted from another's records. Years
    without such a date are corrected exactly as before.

    The library function renames the series it is given in place, so it gets a copy.

    Returns (corrected series, list of years the correction ran in).
    """
    out = series.copy()
    years = sorted({int(y) for y in series.index.year[series > 100]})
    segment = sum(np.asarray(series.index >= _d, dtype=int) for _d in PROBE_STATE_CHANGES)
    for year in years:
        for part in np.unique(segment):
            locs = (series.index.year == year) & (segment == part)
            if not (series.loc[locs] > 100).any():
                continue
            out.loc[locs] = dv.corrections.remove_relativehumidity_offset(
                series.loc[locs].copy(), showplot=showplot)
    return out, years


def monthly_offset_table(target: pd.Series, reference: pd.Series, era: pd.Series,
                         window_years: int = None, min_samples: int = None) -> pd.Series:
    """Additive offset `target - reference` per (era, year, month), fitted on a local year window.

    The offset varies seasonally (about +3.9 % RH in February, +0.4 % RH in August), drifts over
    the record (annual mean +0.08 % RH in 2006, +5.06 % RH in 2017; a pooled offset would apply
    +3.42 % RH to January 2004 where the local evidence gives +1.35 % RH), and steps by 2 to 4
    percentage points at the January 2016 probe replacement. `era` labels the sensor generation of
    every record and each era is fitted only on its own overlap, so no record is reconstructed
    with a relationship from across the break (the failure notebook 02 documents for gap-filling
    models).

    Within an era, each month of each year is fitted on the overlap within +/- `window_years`
    calendar years. A month with fewer than `min_samples` local records falls back to the era's
    pooled monthly offset, a month absent from the era's overlap to its pooled mean. An era with
    no overlap raises instead of returning a table of NaNs.

    Returns a Series indexed by (era, year, month).
    """
    window_years = OFFSET_WINDOW_YEARS if window_years is None else window_years
    min_samples = OFFSET_MIN_SAMPLES if min_samples is None else min_samples

    years = range(int(target.index.year.min()), int(target.index.year.max()) + 1)
    rows = {}
    for era_code in sorted(pd.unique(era)):
        _in_era = (era == era_code).reindex(target.index).fillna(False)
        overlap = pd.concat([target.where(_in_era).rename('t'), reference.rename('r')],
                            axis=1).dropna()
        if overlap.empty:
            raise ValueError(
                f"sensor era {era_code!r} has no overlap between the two series, so no offset can "
                f"be fitted for it. Reconstructing its records from another era's offset is "
                f"exactly what this split exists to prevent.")
        diff = overlap.t - overlap.r
        pooled_monthly = diff.groupby(diff.index.month).mean()
        pooled_mean = float(diff.mean())
        for year in years:
            near = diff[(diff.index.year >= year - window_years) &
                        (diff.index.year <= year + window_years)]
            grouped = near.groupby(near.index.month)
            local_mean, local_n = grouped.mean(), grouped.size()
            for month in range(1, 13):
                if month in local_mean.index and local_n.get(month, 0) >= min_samples:
                    rows[(era_code, year, month)] = float(local_mean[month])
                elif month in pooled_monthly.index:
                    rows[(era_code, year, month)] = float(pooled_monthly[month])
                else:
                    rows[(era_code, year, month)] = pooled_mean
    return pd.Series(rows).rename('offset')


def reconstruct_from_reference(target: pd.Series, reference: pd.Series, offsets: pd.Series,
                               era: pd.Series):
    """Fill gaps in `target` with offset-corrected `reference` values, clipped to [0, 100].

    Each record takes the offset fitted for **its own** sensor era, so a record on one side of the
    January 2016 probe replacement is never reconstructed with a relationship fitted on the other.

    Only records where `target` is missing AND `reference` has a value are touched; everything
    else comes back untouched. Returns (reconstructed series, boolean mask of what was filled).
    """
    filled = target.isna() & reference.notna()
    keys = pd.MultiIndex.from_arrays([era[filled].values,
                                      target.index[filled].year, target.index[filled].month])
    applied = offsets.reindex(keys).values
    if np.isnan(applied).any():
        raise ValueError("no offset available for some records to reconstruct")

    out = target.copy()
    out.loc[filled] = np.clip(reference.loc[filled].values + applied, 0, 100)
    return out, filled

✴️ Corrections

Order of the correction steps

  1. Timestamp shift (August 2012). Later windows refer to the corrected axis.
  2. Removal of the three damaged 2012 periods and of the two probe half-hours of May 2025 and June 2026. Some of the 2012 records read above 100%.
  3. RH > 100% offset correction. Its offset is the daily mean of the records above 100%, so the damaged records must be gone first; otherwise they would set the offset subtracted from good data on neighbouring days. Step 2 reports how many removed records exceeded 100%.

The two reconstruction passes (NABEL, then MeteoSwiss Lägern) run last, so that every gap exists and the offsets are fitted on corrected 47 m data. Step 2 creates gaps, so their provenance is recorded before step 3; step 3 is checked not to write into gaps, as the correction in notebook 03 does.

RH > 100% correction

Values above 100% indicate sensor drift near saturation. dv.corrections.remove_relativehumidity_offset derives a daily offset from the records above 100%, subtracts it from the series and clips what still exceeds 100%.

Scope. The library ffill/bfills the offset over the whole index, so one call on the full record would also shift years without any exceedance: +0.71 % over 2004-2006 and +0.133 % from 2016 to the end of the record. remove_rh_offset_by_year runs it per exceedance year; other years come through bit-identical. A year holding the June 2026 probe change is also split at HV_START, so one probe’s offset is never applied to the other.

Guarantees

  • RH <= 100 in every year: clipped where the correction runs, true by construction elsewhere. The export check asserts it.
  • RH >= 0 is not guaranteed, because the lower end is not clipped. With offsets of at most 2.0 % it does not occur here, and the export check asserts it.
  • A saturated sensor is not repaired: resolution lost near 100% is not recovered.

def load_redactions(path=None) -> dict:
    """Load the name -> pseudonym map.

    Raises if the map is missing. That is the point: carrying on with redaction quietly disabled
    prints real names into a published page and looks exactly like success.
    """
    path = REDACT_MAP if path is None else Path(path)
    if not path.is_file():
        raise FileNotFoundError(
            f"the redaction map is missing: {path}. Without it the names these files record would "
            f"print unredacted. Restore the file rather than skipping this.")
    tab = pd.read_csv(path, encoding='utf-8')
    missing = {'name', 'pseudonym'}.difference(tab.columns)
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"{path}: missing column(s) {sorted(missing)}.")
    pairs = {str(n).strip(): str(p).strip()
             for n, p in zip(tab['name'], tab['pseudonym']) if str(n).strip()}
    if not pairs:
        raise ValueError(f"{path}: holds no usable name -> pseudonym pairs.")
    return pairs

def audit_unmapped_names(texts, label='fieldbook', write_report: bool = True) -> int:
    """Raise if any name-shaped token survived redaction.

    A newer export can name someone the map has never seen. Candidates are never printed - an
    exception message is stored in a notebook like any other output - so they go to a file beside
    the map and only the count is shown.
    """
    allow = set(pd.read_csv(ALLOW_LIST, encoding='utf-8')['term'].astype(str).str.strip())
    found = {}
    for _t in texts:
        for _rx in _NAME_SHAPES:
            for _m in _rx.finditer(str(_t)):
                for _tok in re.findall(_NAME_TOKEN, _m.group(1)):
                    if _tok.isupper() or any(ch.isdigit() for ch in _tok) or _tok in allow:
                        continue
                    found[_tok] = found.get(_tok, 0) + 1
    if found:
        _report = unmapped_report_path(label)
        if write_report:
            _report.write_text(
                '\n'.join(f'{n}\t{t}' for t, n in sorted(found.items(), key=lambda kv: -kv[1])),
                encoding='utf-8')
        raise ValueError(
            f"{label}: {len(found)} name-shaped token(s) survived redaction. They are deliberately "
            f"NOT shown here - see {_report}. Add the people to redact_names.csv and the "
            f"technical terms to redact_allow.csv, then re-run.")
    print(f"  {label}: no unmapped name-shaped tokens ({len(allow)} allowlisted terms)")
    return 0


def program_excerpt(path, pattern: str, before: int = 6, after: int = 4, expect: int = 1) -> str:
    """The lines of a logger program around `pattern`, redacted.

    Only the matched instruction is returned, never the file header: these programs are signed by
    their authors. Raises unless the pattern matches exactly `expect` times. Both directions
    matter: no match reads as 'the program does not measure this', and an over-broad pattern
    quietly prints the wrong instruction next to the argument it is supposed to support.
    """
    lines = Path(path).read_text(encoding='latin-1').splitlines()
    blocks = []
    for i, line in enumerate(lines):
        if re.search(pattern, line):
            lo, hi = max(0, i - before), min(len(lines), i + after + 1)
            blocks.append('\n'.join(lines[lo:hi]))
    if len(blocks) != expect:
        raise ValueError(f"{Path(path).name}: {pattern!r} matched {len(blocks)} time(s), expected "
                         f"{expect}. The program layout has changed, the wrong file is configured, "
                         f"or the pattern is not specific enough.")
    return redact_people('\n\n'.join(blocks))


def calibration_constants(excerpt: str) -> dict:
    """The multiplier and offset of one measurement instruction, parsed from its own text.

    The CR10X lists the two numbers as numbered parameters of a `Volt (SE)` instruction; the
    CR1000 passes them as the last two arguments of a `VoltSe(...)` or `VoltDiff(...)` call. Both
    shapes are matched on their own terms.

    Raises if either number cannot be found. That failure path is not a defect here, it is the
    argument: the CR1000 reads this sensor over **SDI-12**, which returns finished engineering
    units, so its instruction carries no multiplier and no offset at all and this function raises
    on it. A version that returned an empty result instead would let the two eras be compared by
    having nothing to disagree about.
    """
    if 'Mult' in excerpt:                                    # CR10X parameter listing
        _m = re.search(r'^\s*\d+:\s*([\d.]+)\s+Mult', excerpt, re.M)
        _o = re.search(r'^\s*\d+:\s*([\d.]+)\s+Offset', excerpt, re.M)
        tokens = [_m.group(1) if _m else None, _o.group(1) if _o else None]
    else:                                                    # CR1000 VoltSe/VoltDiff call
        _call = re.search(r'Volt(?:Se|Diff)\s*\(([^)]*)\)', excerpt)
        _args = [a.strip() for a in _call.group(1).split(',')] if _call else []
        tokens = _args[-2:] if len(_args) >= 2 else [None, None]
    values = []
    for _tok in tokens:
        if _tok is None:
            raise ValueError("could not parse a multiplier and an offset from the instruction - "
                             "the program language or the layout has changed, or the measurement "
                             "is not an analog voltage at all")
        values.append(float(_tok))
    return {'multiplier': values[0], 'offset': values[1]}

# points of RH (the ratio machinery of notebooks 01 and 03 is for radiation). Controls in the helper:
#   Season: the difference has a seasonal cycle (about +3.9 % RH in February, +0.4 % RH in August),
#     so medians are taken per calendar month and then averaged over months.
#   Sample size: a month with fewer than MIN_RECORDS half-hours is dropped; a pair with no

def diff_by_year(a: str, b: str, mask: pd.Series = None, min_records: int = MIN_RECORDS
                 ) -> pd.Series:
    """Month-matched yearly mean of `a - b`, in percentage points of RH."""
    _j = rh3.loc[rh3.index.year <= LAST_FULL_YEAR, [a, b]].dropna()
    if mask is not None:
        _j = _j[mask.reindex(_j.index).fillna(False)]
    _d = _j[a] - _j[b]
    _m = _d.groupby([_d.index.year, _d.index.month]).median()
    _n = _d.groupby([_d.index.year, _d.index.month]).count()
    _m = _m[_n >= min_records]
    if _m.empty:
        raise ValueError(f"{a} - {b}: no calendar month reaches {min_records} records, so a yearly "
                         f"level cannot be built from this selection")
    return _m.groupby(level=0).mean()

def step_at(series: pd.Series, year: int, span: int = 3) -> float:
    """Change of a yearly level across `year`, over `span` years either side.

    The candidate year itself is excluded from both sides: a change inside a year belongs to
    neither era. Returns nan when either side is thin, so a year at the edge of the record cannot
    win by having almost no data behind it.
    """
    _pre = series.reindex(range(year - span, year)).dropna()
    _post = series.reindex(range(year + 1, year + 1 + span)).dropna()
    if len(_pre) < 2 or len(_post) < 2:
        return float('nan')
    return float(_post.mean() - _pre.mean())

🔟 A transfer across 2016: fitted on the era means it does not hold, fitted at the break it does

For a step that is not one number, the homogenisation is a transfer that varies with the humidity: per humidity bin, the difference between the eras, added to the earlier era. This step first fits it against MeteoSwiss Lägern on the era means (2005-2015 against 2017 to CS215_LAST_NORMAL_YEAR, the last full year before the CS215 changed state, step 1️⃣1️⃣) and shows that this estimate is not stable, because both eras drift. It then fits the transfer against NABEL at the break, on the three years before it and the CS215 record up to the end of NABEL’s independent period, shows that this estimate depends little on the window, and scores it against Lägern, which entered no fit. This is the arrangement notebook 02 uses for TA. RH_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED uses the transfer fitted at the break: it removes the step at the break and leaves the drift within each era.

Every fit uses measured records only, binned on the reference humidity; binning on the tower’s reading would produce a slope from regression to the mean.

def transfer_curve(frame, pre_years, post_years, min_n=1500):
    """The correction a level-dependent homogenisation would apply, per humidity bin.

    Returns each era's mean tower-minus-reference difference and their difference, which is what
    would have to be ADDED to the earlier era to put it on the later era's level. Bins holding
    fewer than `min_n` records in either era are dropped rather than reported as noise.
    """
    def _binned(sel):
        _b = pd.cut(sel['ref'], bins=HOM_EDGES, labels=HOM_MID, include_lowest=True, right=False)
        _g = (sel['tower'] - sel['ref']).groupby(_b, observed=False)
        return _g.mean(), _g.count()

    _mp, _npre = _binned(frame[frame.index.year.isin(pre_years)])
    _mq, _npost = _binned(frame[frame.index.year.isin(post_years)])
    _t = pd.DataFrame({'pre_diff': _mp, 'post_diff': _mq, 'delta': _mq - _mp,
                       'n_pre': _npre, 'n_post': _npost})
    return _t[(_t.n_pre >= min_n) & (_t.n_post >= min_n)]

The transfer at the break, fitted against NABEL

NABEL measures at 49 m on the same tower with its own logger and housing, so it sees the same air as the 47 m probe; MeteoSwiss Lägern is 2.5 km away. The transfer is fitted on tower minus NABEL with the binning of transfer_curve(): the MP101A over the three full years before the break (LOCAL_PRE_YEARS), the CS215 from the first full month after it (LOCAL_POST_START) to the end of NABEL’s independent period (LOCAL_POST_END, step 3️⃣). Close to the break the drift has had little time to act. Each bin’s correction is attached to the MP101A’s own reading in that bin (tower_reading), interpolated between bins and held constant beyond the outer bins, so it can be applied to an MP101A value. Near saturation, where both probes sit at the ceiling, the fitted step is close to zero; the top bin keeps its own small correction, and the result is capped to [0, 100], so a saturated MP101A record stays at 100 %.

Three checks follow: the transfer must come out the same from shorter windows; applied to the MP101A era, it must remove the step against Lägern, which entered no fit (fit against one reference, score against another); and the same transfer fitted against Lägern is printed beside it, because the difference between the two is the reference dependence of this correction.

def pre_window(frame, years=None):
    """The MP101A window: full calendar years before the break."""
    return frame[frame.index.year.isin(LOCAL_PRE_YEARS if years is None else years)]


def post_window(frame, start=None, end=None):
    """The CS215 window: from `start` to before `end`."""
    start = LOCAL_POST_START if start is None else start
    end = LOCAL_POST_END if end is None else end
    return frame[(frame.index >= start) & (frame.index < end)]


def binned_transfer(pre: pd.DataFrame, post: pd.DataFrame, min_n: int = None) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """The binning of transfer_curve() on two explicit windows of a (tower, ref) frame.

    Per reference-humidity bin: each window's mean tower - reference difference, their difference
    `delta` (what is added to the earlier era), the record counts, and `tower_reading`, the
    earlier era's own reading in that bin. Bins holding fewer than `min_n` records in either window
    are dropped; raises if none is left.
    """
    min_n = LOCAL_MIN_N if min_n is None else min_n

    def _binned(sel):
        _b = pd.cut(sel['ref'], bins=HOM_EDGES, labels=HOM_MID, include_lowest=True, right=False)
        _g = (sel['tower'] - sel['ref']).groupby(_b, observed=False)
        return _g.mean(), _g.count()

    (_a, _na), (_p, _np) = _binned(pre), _binned(post)
    _t = pd.DataFrame({'pre_diff': _a, 'post_diff': _p, 'delta': _p - _a, 'n_pre': _na, 'n_post': _np})
    _t = _t[(_t.n_pre >= min_n) & (_t.n_post >= min_n)]
    if _t.empty:
        raise ValueError(f"no humidity bin holds {min_n} records in both windows")
    _t['tower_reading'] = _t.index.to_series().astype(float) + _t['pre_diff']
    return _t

def mp101a_to_cs215(rh_mp, curve=None):
    """CS215 equivalent of an MP101A reading, from the 2016 transfer of step 10.

    The correction is interpolated on the MP101A reading between the bins and held constant beyond
    the outer bins (np.interp). The result is capped to [0, 100]; NaN stays NaN.
    """
    curve = LOCAL_CURVE if curve is None else curve
    _v = np.asarray(rh_mp, dtype=float)
    _c = curve['correction'] if 'correction' in curve else curve['delta']
    _out = np.clip(_v + np.interp(_v, curve['tower_reading'].values, _c.values), 0.0, 100.0)

    if isinstance(rh_mp, pd.Series):
        return pd.Series(_out, index=rh_mp.index, name=rh_mp.name)
    return _out

What step 🔟 settles

Fitted on the era means, the transfer is not stable. Estimates from the first and second halves of each era differ by up to the size of the correction, and summer and winter by about as much again. The drift figure shows why: each era travels further over its own length than the step between them, the two in opposite directions, so a transfer fitted on the era means is correct for no particular year.

Fitted at the break against NABEL, it is. Shorter windows change it by less than the year-to-year noise of each bin plus a year of drift, and applied to the MP101A era it removes most of the step against Lägern, which entered no fit, to within Lägern’s own year-to-year change plus the drift the windows contain. RH_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED raises the MP101A era by this transfer. The shorter windows give a smaller correction in every bin from 40 to 85 % (asserted), so part of what the fitted windows attribute to the probes may be drift.

The correction depends on the reference. Fitted against Lägern over the same windows, it raises the MP101A records by several tenths of a percentage point more on average and by up to about 2.4 percentage points more just below saturation, where NABEL finds almost no step and Lägern a large one (table above). That difference is the uncertainty of the homogenised column across 2016, and it is largest in humid conditions.

The drift is not corrected. It is unattributed, and after mid-2018 no third series exists to attribute it (NABEL stops, then repeats the tower). Fitting the tower onto MeteoSwiss would replace the measurement with the reference, which 01 and 03 decline for an unattributed drift. The homogenised column is therefore free of the step at the break and not homogeneous over decades: against Lägern the MP101A era drifts by DRIFT_PRE_PER_YR and the CS215 era by DRIFT_POST_PER_YR (printed above, in percentage points a year, from January to September of each year so that the partial year 2024 compares with the others), in opposite directions, and a trend computed from either column carries that drift.

def es_hpa(ta):
    """Saturation vapour pressure over water (hPa) at air temperature `ta` (degC).

    Magnus form with the coefficients of diive's calc_vpd_from_ta_rh (6.1078 hPa, 17.08085,
    234.175 degC), the formula notebook 07 computes VPD with.
    """
    _t = np.asarray(ta, dtype=float)
    _out = 6.1078 * np.exp(17.08085 * _t / (234.175 + _t))
    return pd.Series(_out, index=ta.index) if isinstance(ta, pd.Series) else _out

What step 1️⃣1️⃣ settles

The change of 16 May 2025 is a change of the probe’s temperature and not of its humidity element: in sun, RH against Lägern moves across the handling by more than in any normal year, while the vapour pressure moves by a small fraction of what that RH change would mean (table above). RH_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED therefore leaves codes 6 and 7 as measured, as it leaves code 0. A correction of these records would be a correction of the probe temperature, which belongs to notebook 02.

Limitations. Both states are probe-heating effects that remain in the exported RH. Code 7 is warmer in sun than the normal CS215 and reports its humidity relative to that temperature, so it reads low in sun against the summers just before it; code 6 is cooler in sun, because the daytime excess of the normal CS215 is gone as well, and reads a higher RH in sun than every normal CS215 summer on the same dates. Codes 6 and 7 are excluded from every statistic meant to describe the normal CS215: the era-mean transfer of step 🔟 (full years to CS215_LAST_NORMAL_YEAR), its drift (January to September of each year, through the last record before EXCESS_START), the screening-splice comparison of step 8️⃣ and the CS215 reference of step 1️⃣2️⃣. Each state has its own reconstruction offsets.

1️⃣2️⃣ The June 2026 probe change

What changed. On 5 June 2026 a Campbell HygroVUE10 (SDI-12, SN E6730) replaced the CS215 at 47 m. The fieldbook adds it at this location on that day and records a change of its SDI-12 configuration ID together with a new logger program on 11 June (step 9️⃣); no entry records the removal of the CS215. The data date the swap to the half-hour: the raw 1-min TA_T1_47_1 stops at 11:10 and resumes at 11:31, and TA_T1_47_1 and RH_T1_47_1 both change level against MeteoSwiss Lägern from that day. Field name and database tags are unchanged. HV_START (11:45, TIMESTAMP_MID) is the first HygroVUE10 record; the swap half-hour before it was removed in the corrections.

Why vapour pressure. Step 1️⃣1️⃣ shows that this probe’s RH in sun follows its temperature, and the two probes differ in their radiation behaviour (notebook 02: the HygroVUE10 reads cooler in sun). A transfer in %RH would mix the humidity elements with the temperature sensors. The transfer is therefore made in vapour pressure e, which a temperature error leaves in place, and converted back to RH with 02’s homogenised temperature.

Method.

  1. e from each sensor’s own RH and TA, as in step 1️⃣1️⃣: tower RH_T1_47_1 with 02’s measured TA_T1_47_1_gfXG, Lägern RH_LAE_MS with TA_LAE_MS.
  2. Two samples, matched on calendar dates (12 June to 30 September): the HygroVUE10 from HV_FIT_START to PERIOD_END, after the configuration change, so 5-11 June are excluded as transitional; and the normal CS215 (code 0) over the same dates of HV_CS215_YEARS, the summers before its excess heating, as in 02. Measured records only, everything present.
  3. The difference d_e = e_47 - e_LAE is binned on e_LAE and split by day and night. If the HygroVUE10-minus-CS215 difference varies across the bins by more than the CS215 year-to-year scatter, each sample is fitted as d_e = a + b * e_LAE and the difference of the two lines is applied through the e_LAE a HygroVUE10 value corresponds to, held inside the fitted range; otherwise it is a constant. The decision is computed below.
  4. RH_HOM = 100 * (e_47 - Delta_e) / es(TA_HOM), where Delta_e is the HygroVUE10-minus-CS215 difference and TA_HOM is 02’s homogenised temperature of the same record, capped to [0, 100].

What the homogenised RH refers to. From June 2026 it is relative to 02’s homogenised air temperature, not to a CS215’s own temperature. 02 removes the CS215 shield’s radiative excess, so in sun the homogenised RH lies above what the CS215 would have read; at night the two agree. The amount is computed below.

Limitations. The transfer rests on one summer and on one reference: NABEL ended in 2018, so Lägern is the only series to fit against, and a month hold-out takes the place of a second one. It is fitted on June to September and applied as fitted to the whole HygroVUE10 era, including the transitional week. Its month hold-out residuals follow the season, negative in June and July, near zero in August and positive in September (table below): within the summer the transfer leaves the early months slightly too dry and the late ones slightly too wet, and its behaviour outside the summer is untested. The hold-out tolerance is pooled over the months, because three CS215 summers cannot estimate one month’s scatter reliably. It depends on 02: the measured TA enters e, the homogenised TA the conversion back, so 02 runs before this notebook.

def fit_e_line(sample: pd.DataFrame, label: str, linear: bool = None, min_records: int = None) -> dict:
    """Fit `d_e = a + b * e_LAE` (linear) or `d_e = a` (constant) on one sample.

    Raises when the sample holds fewer than `min_records` records or spans less than 5 hPa of
    Lägern vapour pressure: a fit on a few days describes those days' weather, not the probe.
    """
    linear = HV_E_LINEAR if linear is None else linear
    min_records = HV_MIN_RECORDS if min_records is None else min_records
    _s = sample[['e_ref', 'd_e']].dropna()
    if len(_s) < min_records:
        raise ValueError(f"{label}: {len(_s)} records, fewer than the {min_records} a transfer is fitted on")
    _span = float(_s['e_ref'].max() - _s['e_ref'].min())
    if _span < 5:
        raise ValueError(f"{label}: the records span only {_span:.1f} hPa of Lägern vapour pressure")
    if linear:
        _b, _a = np.polyfit(_s['e_ref'].values, _s['d_e'].values, 1)
    else:
        _a, _b = float(_s['d_e'].mean()), 0.0
    return {'a': float(_a), 'b': float(_b), 'lowest Lägern e (hPa)': float(_s['e_ref'].min()),
            'highest Lägern e (hPa)': float(_s['e_ref'].max()), 'records': len(_s)}


def hv_e_to_cs215(e_hv, fit_cs: dict, fit_hv: dict):
    """CS215 equivalent of a HygroVUE10 vapour pressure: `e_hv - Delta_e`.

    The value is mapped to the Lägern vapour pressure it corresponds to, held inside the range both
    fits cover, and corrected by the difference of the two fitted lines there. NaN stays NaN.
    """
    _v = np.asarray(e_hv, dtype=float)
    _x = (_v - fit_hv['a']) / (1 + fit_hv['b'])
    _x = np.clip(_x, max(fit_cs['lowest Lägern e (hPa)'], fit_hv['lowest Lägern e (hPa)']),
                 min(fit_cs['highest Lägern e (hPa)'], fit_hv['highest Lägern e (hPa)']))
    _out = _v + (fit_cs['a'] - fit_hv['a']) + (fit_cs['b'] - fit_hv['b']) * _x
    return pd.Series(_out, index=e_hv.index, name=e_hv.name) if isinstance(e_hv, pd.Series) else _out


def hv_to_cs215_rh(rh_hv, ta_measured, ta_homogenized, fit_cs: dict = None, fit_hv: dict = None):
    """Homogenised RH of a HygroVUE10 record: its vapour pressure (own RH and TA) on the CS215 level,
    relative to notebook 02's homogenised TA of the same record, capped to [0, 100]."""
    fit_cs = FIT_CS215 if fit_cs is None else fit_cs
    fit_hv = FIT_HV if fit_hv is None else fit_hv
    _e = np.asarray(rh_hv, dtype=float) / 100 * es_hpa(np.asarray(ta_measured, dtype=float))
    _out = np.clip(100 * hv_e_to_cs215(_e, fit_cs, fit_hv) / es_hpa(np.asarray(ta_homogenized, dtype=float)),
                   0.0, 100.0)
    return pd.Series(_out, index=rh_hv.index, name=rh_hv.name) if isinstance(rh_hv, pd.Series) else _out

What step 1️⃣2️⃣ settles

From HV_START the column holds a third probe. In vapour pressure against Lägern the HygroVUE10 reads drier than the normal CS215, by more than the CS215 summers differ among themselves. Hence:

  • the HygroVUE10 era has its own sensor code (4) and its own reconstruction offsets, and the swap half-hour has code 5 and belongs to the code 6 era;
  • RH_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED carries the HygroVUE10 era with its vapour pressure on the CS215 level, converted back with 02’s homogenised temperature. In vapour pressure the transfer meets the tolerances asserted above, and the month hold-out supports it on months it was not fitted on, within a tolerance pooled over the months; its residuals follow the season.

In relative humidity the homogenised HygroVUE10 era is not on the level of the CS215’s own reading in sun: it refers to 02’s homogenised temperature, which carries no radiative excess, while the CS215’s RH is relative to its warm probe (table above). At night the two agree. The transfer is fitted on one summer and applied unchanged to later months; it is to be refitted once the HygroVUE10 record includes a winter.

What this section settles

RH_T1_47_1 is three instruments in one column: a Rotronic MP101A from September 2004 to December 2015, a Campbell CS215 from 21 January 2016, and a Campbell HygroVUE10 from 5 June 2026. At the first change the column steps upwards by 2.1 to 3.3 percentage points and 3.9 to 4.1 away from saturation. That step is on the tower probe and present at every hour, and the replaced probe had drifted past 100 % in 3.6 % of its last full year. The CS215 changed state twice before it was replaced: from 22 October 2024 it heated abnormally in sun, and after its handling on 16 May 2025 its daytime excess was gone; in RH both are effects of the probe temperature (step 1️⃣1️⃣). At the second probe change the column becomes drier in vapour pressure (step 1️⃣2️⃣). Hence:

  • FLAG_RH_T1_47_1_SOURCE names the probe generation and state of every record, with the codes of 02, so the field name does not imply a continuity the hardware lacks.
  • Reconstruction offsets are fitted within each era and probe state, five since June 2026. A relationship across a change would put reconstructed records on neither side’s level, the failure 02 documents for gap-filling models. At the 2016 change this concerns about a thousand records, almost all in the January 2016 outage, moved by up to 1.8 % RH.
  • RH_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED puts the other probes on the CS215 level. No constant fits the 2016 step, which is 3 to 6 percentage points in the drier half of the range and at most 1.1 above 95 % RH, so the MP101A era is raised by a humidity-dependent transfer. Fitted on the era means that transfer is unstable, because both eras drift; fitted against NABEL close to the break it is stable and removes most of the step against Lägern (step 🔟). The HygroVUE10 era is transferred in vapour pressure, fitted on one summer (step 1️⃣2️⃣). The CS215 itself is left as measured in all three states.
  • The 2016 correction depends on the reference. Fitted against Lägern it would be larger by several tenths of a percentage point on average and by up to about 2.4 just below saturation, the uncertainty of the homogenised column across 2016.
  • The drift within the eras and the probe heating of 2024-2025 are not corrected. The drift is unattributed after NABEL stops in 2018, and fitting it away would replace the measurement with the reference; the heating is a temperature error, which belongs to 02.
WarningKnown limitation of this product

Any statistic from RH_T1_47_1 that crosses 21 January 2016 or 5 June 2026 contains a probe step: period means, trends, year rankings and anomalies over the full record. Use RH_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED for these, or stay within one era (filter on FLAG_RH_T1_47_1_SOURCE).

The homogenised column is not homogeneous over decades. Against MeteoSwiss the MP101A era drifts downwards and the CS215 era upwards, each by a few tenths of a percentage point a year (step 🔟), so a trend from either column, and a comparison of years far from the break, still contains sensor movement. Across 2016 the homogenised column carries the reference dependence of its correction, largest in humid conditions. In sun, codes 7 and 6 carry the probe’s heating: code 7 reads low, code 6 higher than the normal CS215, in both columns. From June 2026 the homogenised RH refers to 02’s homogenised temperature and lies above the CS215’s own level in sun.

Quantities computed from RH at low humidity, VPD above all, inherit more of the step. Notebook 07 computes VPD from the measured series and the homogenised TA and measures a residual step of about 17 % of the mean. With the uncorrected TA it would appear about three times smaller, because the two input errors have opposite signs on VPD, a masking not to be relied on.

🟩 Reconstruction from the NABEL sensor

Why, and what this is not

RH_T1_47_1 begins on 20 September 2004, which leaves an 8.7-month block without measurement, besides scattered gaps and the removed 2012 periods. NABEL at 49 m on the same tower measured throughout 2004-2018. Its values are transferred, not modelled (no drivers, no XGBoost, unlike 01-03): a long block such as 2004 is what a driver-based model handles worst and a co-located sensor handles well. Records without a NABEL value, chiefly after 2018, are filled in a second pass from MeteoSwiss Lägern.

The two sensors do not differ by a constant

🟩 Second reconstruction pass: MeteoSwiss Lägern

NABEL ends in 2018. The gaps it leaves, almost all after 2018 and counted below, are filled from MeteoSwiss Lägern (RH_LAE_MS, 2.5 km, 845 m, 2004-2026) in the same way: a per-month offset fitted on the tower-Lägern overlap within each sensor era and probe state, added to the Lägern value. There are five: the MP101A, the normal CS215, its two states of 2024-2025 and the HygroVUE10, each fitted on its own overlap. Each probe half-hour is reconstructed with the offset of the era before it.

Lägern is a weaker reference than NABEL. It correlates with tower RH at r = 0.94 (alignment guard above), and its offset varies seasonally, steps at the probe replacement and includes a real altitude effect, which the monthly offset absorbs. Its fills carry flag 4, distinct from NABEL’s 3.

⚠️ Note for downstream notebooks

This product is complete. FLAG_RH_T1_47_1_MISSING:

  • 0: measured at 47 m and corrected, the only genuine measurements of this sensor.
  • 3: reconstructed from NABEL at 49 m on the same tower, about 3 % RH uncertainty.
  • 4: reconstructed from MeteoSwiss Lägern, same method, larger uncertainty; almost all after
  • 1 / 2: in the legend, but do not occur.

For uncertainty estimates, or fits with RH as the independent variable, consider excluding 3 and 4.

Which value column to use.

  • Within one sensor era: RH_T1_47_1, the values the probe reported.
  • Across 21 January 2016 or 5 June 2026: RH_T1_47_1_HOMOGENIZED, on the CS215 level. RH_T1_47_1 steps upwards by 2 to 4 percentage points at the first date and becomes drier, increasingly so with the humidity, at the second. The difference between the two columns is not a constant: it depends on the humidity, is zero on the CS215, and is small at saturation before
  • For trends over many years, neither column is sufficient on its own: the drift within each era (step 🔟) is not corrected, so a long trend carries sensor movement.
  • The 2016 correction is fitted against NABEL and scored against Lägern. Fitted against Lägern it would be larger by several tenths of a percentage point on average and by up to about 2.4 just below saturation (step 🔟); treat that as its uncertainty, largest in humid conditions.
  • Codes 7 and 6 (22 October 2024 to 5 June 2026) are left as measured in both columns. In sun code 7 reads low and code 6 higher than the normal CS215, both from the probe’s temperature (step 1️⃣1️⃣); exclude them, or use daily means with care, wherever the sun-dependent part of RH matters.
  • From 5 June 2026 the homogenised RH is relative to 02’s homogenised temperature (step 1️⃣2️⃣), so it lies above what the CS215 read in sun and agrees with it at night. VPD computed from it with 02’s homogenised TA is consistent.

FLAG_RH_T1_47_1_SOURCE carries 02’s codes and is asserted to agree with FLAG_TA_T1_47_1_SOURCE on every record. The HygroVUE10 transfer is fitted on June to September 2026 and applied unchanged to later months. Its month hold-out residuals follow the season (negative in June and July, positive in September, step 1️⃣2️⃣), so monthly means of the homogenised HygroVUE10 era carry a seasonal error of a few tenths of a hPa in vapour pressure.
